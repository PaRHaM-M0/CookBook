Iranian-Style Joojeh Chini

Battered, Deep-Fried Chicken Strips

Joojeh chini, as commonly prepared in Iranian recipes, is chicken coated in a beniye-style batter and fried until crisp. Recipes vary, so this is a practical home version—not a claim that the dish is a traditional Chinese recipe.

Yield
4 servings
Active time
About 45–55 minutes
Marinating time
20–30 minutes
Total time
About 1 hour 5 minutes to 1 hour 25 minutes
Difficulty
Easy to moderate

The main challenge is maintaining the oil temperature and cooking the chicken through without over-browning the coating.

Ingredients

Chicken and marinade
Boneless, skinless chicken breast1¼ lb (600 g)
Finely grated onion2 tablespoons (about 20 g)
Lemon juice1 tablespoon (15 mL)
Garlic clove, finely grated1
Fine salt¾ teaspoon
Black pepper½ teaspoon
Paprika½ teaspoon
Neutral oil1 teaspoon
Saffron water, optional1 teaspoon
Dredge
All-purpose flour¼ cup (30 g)
Cornstarch2 tablespoons (15 g)
Salt and black pepperPinch
Beniye batter
All-purpose flour1 cup (120 g)
Cornstarch¼ cup (30 g)
Baking powder1 teaspoon
Fine salt½ teaspoon
Large egg1
Very cold sparkling water⅔ cup (160 mL)
Additional very cold sparkling water, if neededUp to 2 tablespoons (30 mL)
For frying and serving
Neutral frying oil, or enough to reach 2 inches (5 cm) in a heavy potAbout 1½ quarts (1.4 L)
Lemon wedgesFor serving
Optional quick tartar sauce
Mayonnaise½ cup
Finely chopped pickles2 tablespoons
Lemon juice1 teaspoon
Dijon mustard1 teaspoon
Chopped parsley or dill1 tablespoon

Equipment

  • Cutting board and sharp knife
  • Two mixing bowls
  • Whisk
  • Heavy, deep pot or Dutch oven
  • Clip-on or instant-read thermometer
  • Tongs or a spider skimmer
  • Wire rack set over a tray
Phase 1

Marinate the Chicken

  1. Cut the chicken evenly.

    Slice it into strips about ½ inch (1.25 cm) thick and 3 inches (7–8 cm) long. Keeping the strips a similar thickness helps them cook at roughly the same rate.

  2. Season and chill.

    Combine the onion, lemon juice, garlic, salt, pepper, paprika, oil and optional saffron water. Add the chicken, coat it, cover and refrigerate for 20–30 minutes. The marinade adds flavor; keep raw chicken refrigerated while it marinates.

  3. Prepare the optional sauce.

    Stir together the mayonnaise, pickles, lemon juice, mustard and herbs; refrigerate until serving. Making it ahead keeps the frying stage focused and the finished chicken crisp.

Phase 2

Make the Batter

  1. Mix the dry ingredients.

    In a bowl, whisk the flour, cornstarch, baking powder and salt. In a second bowl, beat the egg with the cold sparkling water.

  2. Combine just before frying.

    Pour the egg mixture into the dry ingredients and whisk briefly, just until no dry pockets remain. A few small lumps are fine; overmixing can make the coating heavier. The batter should resemble thick pancake batter and cling to a strip of chicken. If it is so thick it will not coat, whisk in up to 2 tbsp (30 mL) more cold sparkling water.

  3. Keep it cold.

    Set the batter bowl over a larger bowl of ice while you fry, if convenient. A cold batter is easier to work with and helps preserve a light coating; do not leave it sitting out while the chicken is frying.

Phase 3

Fry the Chicken

  1. Heat the oil safely.

    Pour the oil into a heavy pot, filling it no more than halfway. Heat it to 350°F (175°C); aim to keep it between 340–355°F (170–180°C) while frying. Use a thermometer rather than judging by appearance, and keep water away from the hot oil.

  2. Dredge and batter in small batches.

    Mix the dredge ingredients. Lift a few chicken strips from the marinade, remove visible onion pieces and blot excess moisture. Coat lightly in the dredge and shake off the excess; dip each strip in batter and let the extra drip back into the bowl. The light flour coating helps the wet batter adhere.

  3. Fry without crowding.

    Lower a few battered strips carefully into the oil, one at a time. Fry for about 3–5 minutes, turning as needed, until golden and crisp. Cook in batches so the oil temperature does not fall sharply; let the oil return to the target range between batches.

  4. Check the chicken’s internal temperature.

    Check the thickest strip with a food thermometer. The chicken should reach 165°F (74°C); color alone is not a reliable safety check.

  5. Drain and serve promptly.

    Transfer cooked strips to a wire rack set over a tray, not a stack of paper towels. The rack lets steam escape so the bottoms stay crisp. Serve hot with lemon and the optional tartar sauce.

Troubleshooting

  • The batter slides off: The chicken may be too wet or still have onion pieces attached. Blot it dry, dredge lightly, shake off excess flour, then dip in the batter.
  • The coating is greasy or soft: The oil may be too cool, or the pot may be overcrowded. Fry fewer pieces at a time and let the oil recover between batches.
  • The coating browns before the chicken is cooked: The oil may be too hot or the strips too thick. Lower the heat and cut the next batch more evenly; finish cooking to 165°F (74°C).
  • The batter is heavy or bready: It may have been overmixed or left standing too long. Mix briefly and use it soon after combining.
  • The chicken is cooked but the coating has softened: It may have been covered or stacked while hot. Drain on a wire rack and serve promptly.

Food Safety

Keep the raw chicken and its marinade separate from ready-to-eat foods, and refrigerate during marinating. Discard leftover marinade and batter that contacted raw chicken. Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C), measured with a food thermometer.

Sources and Recipe Notes

  • Iranian recipe references describe versions of joojeh chini using chicken strips and beniye-style batter; their ingredient ratios and flavorings vary.
  • The chicken-strip, dredge-and-batter approach is also similar in technique to Japanese chicken tempura, but that does not establish that joojeh chini originated there.
  • The specific measurements and this no-yeast batter are an editorial synthesis, not a single source’s tested formula. This recipe has not been personally kitchen-tested.
  • USDA FSIS recommends refrigerating poultry during marinating and cooking poultry to 165°F (74°C).
