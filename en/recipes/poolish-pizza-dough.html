72-Hour Poolish Pizza Dough

Phase Zero: Engineering the 72-Hour Master Poolish Dough for Home Ovens. Why build around a Poolish preferment at 71% total hydration? Because a 250°C home oven bakes pizza over 8 to 12 minutes rather than 90 seconds at 450°C in a wood-fired dome, evaporating far more water. Pushing hydration to 71% alongside a 72-hour cold enzyme breakdown produces an airy, honeycomb, blistered Cornicione rim even on steel or stone at home. Switch on your gram scale — every single ingredient here is measured strictly by weight.

Yield
4 to 5 dough balls (~250 g each)
Total Hydration
71% (500 g water / 700 g total flour)
Start Window
72 to 80 hours before bake time
Poolish Cold Rest
16 to 24 hours in fridge
Skill Level
Advanced
Day 1: Poolish Day 2: Main Dough Bulk Cold Ferment (Up to 72h) Using in Neapolitan Pizza

Ingredients

Weigh obsessively to the gram. Total batch yields roughly 4 to 5 balls at ~250 g each.

For the Final Dough (Day 2)
All ripe Poolish from Day 1Entire batch

Blended flour: 300 g very strong Manitoba / high-protein bread flour + 200 g Tipo 00 (or 500 g single high-grade bread flour)500 g
Fine sea salt20 g
Extra-virgin olive oil10 to 15 g

Only keeping one bag of flour in the pantry? Use quality Tipo 00 pizza flour or unbleached bread flour with 11.5–12.5%+ protein.

Day 1

Building the Poolish — The Soul of Your Crust

Start 72–80h pre-bake — 1h room-temp wake-up, then strictly 16–24h refrigerated

  1. In a roomy, clear, lidded glass or food-safe plastic tub (leave generous headspace for expansion!), whisk together 200 g room-temperature water, yeast (2 g dry or 5 g fresh), and 5 g honey with a fork until fully dissolved — honey jump-starts yeast fermentation smoothly.

  2. Add 200 g flour right into the liquid.

  3. Stir briskly with a fork just until zero dry flour pockets remain. You are NOT making firm dough yet — expect a loose, sticky, pancake-batter slurry.

  4. Leave uncovered on the kitchen counter for 1 hour so yeast activity kicks off visibly, then snap the airtight lid shut and refrigerate for strictly 16 to 24 hours. This slow cold preferment builds complex organic aromas and delicate sweetness without harsh sourness.

Day 2

Mixing & Developing the Final Dough

Pull Poolish 1h early — 10 to 15 mins Slap & Fold until full Windowpane

  1. 5. Waking the Ripe Poolish

    Inspect your tub: gentle bakery fermentation aromas, faint floral alcohol notes, and a domed surface webbed with thousands of tiny honeycomb bubbles confirm peak yeast vitality. Pull the tub out onto the counter for 1 full hour to take off the chill.

  2. 6. Loading the Mixing Bowl

    Scrape every single gram of bubbly poolish into a large mixing bowl.

  3. 7. Dissolving Salt in Cold Water

    In a separate pitcher, dissolve the 20 g fine sea salt completely in 300 g cold water — cold water keeps dough temperature down during hand kneading so fermentation never runs wild prematurely.

  4. 8. Incorporating Flour & Salt Water

    Add half the salted cold water along with the remaining 500 g flour into the poolish bowl, mixing by hand and streaming in the rest of the salted water gradually until all flour pulls cleanly off the bowl walls into a shaggy mass.

  5. 9. Emulsifying Olive Oil Last

    Drizzle the 10 to 15 g extra-virgin olive oil over the shaggy dough right inside the bowl and squeeze it in thoroughly. Oil always enters last so fat never coats dry flour strands before hydration. While strict wood-fired AVPN rules forbid oil at 450°C, Italian home-oven masters rely on this 1.5–2% olive oil to lock interior tenderness while crisping the crust across an 8–12 minute bake.

  6. 10. 10–15 Minute Slap & Fold to Windowpane

    Turn the wet dough onto an unfloured counter and work continuously for 10 to 15 minutes using rhythmic Slap and Fold motions — lifting, slapping down onto the counter, stretching forward, and folding over itself. Yes, 71% dough feels sticky at first: do NOT dust extra flour into it! Lightly wet your hands instead. Watch it transform into a smooth, satiny ball that passes the Windowpane Test — stretching paper-thin without tearing.

Days 2–3

Bulk Cold Fermentation Up to 72 Hours

1h ambient rest with stretch-and-folds, then straight into the cold bottom fridge shelf until bake day

  1. 11. Ambient Rest & Gluten Folds

    Return the smooth dough to a lightly covered bowl at room temperature for 1 full hour, performing gentle coil / stretch-and-fold sets just as detailed in our Neapolitan guide to lock final tension.

  2. 12. Cold Transfer

    With lightly dampened hands, ease the tensioned bulk dough mass into a wide, lightly oiled, airtight container and slide immediately onto the coldest lower shelf of your refrigerator.

  3. 13. Cold Maturation Until Bake Day

    Leave undisturbed in cold dormancy across the remainder of the 72-hour window until bake day, when you'll ball, relax, stretch, and bake.

Master Baker's Formula Summary Total flour: 700 g · Total water: 500 g (71.4% hydration) · Fine sea salt: 20 g (2.85%) · Honey: 5 g · Extra-virgin olive oil: 10–15 g · Yeast: 5 g fresh / 2 g dry — yielding 4 to 5 ~250 g pizzas.
Extended Storage

Holding Longer in the Fridge vs. Long-Term Freezing

Fridge only buys +24h slack; true multi-week storage means freezing portioned dough balls

  1. A) Bake Delayed by Hours up to 1 Day: Refrigerate Balls

    Because enzymatic breakdown began back on Day 1 in the poolish, the refrigerator is NOT indefinite storage. If you miss Day 3, ball the dough into ~250 g portions, oil lightly, seal individually at 4°C, and bake within at most +24 hours past the 72-hour mark (~96h total from poolish start). Sharp acetone/boozy smells, slack puddles, and instant tearing mean gluten has over-proteolyzed past its peak.

  2. B) True Long-Term Storage: Flash-Freezing Dough Balls

    Freeze right after Day 2's 1-hour ambient post-knead rest — before running the full 72-hour cold ferment. Divide into lightly oiled 250 g balls, flash-freeze uncovered on a parchment tray until solid, wrap each ball tightly in plastic wrap, and seal inside labeled freezer zip bags with zero trapped air. Peak oven spring holds for 1 month (usable up to 3 months with gradual yeast attenuation).

  3. C) Proper Thawing & Proofing Protocol

    Transfer wrapped frozen balls to the refrigerator 12 to 24 hours ahead (the night before) to thaw slowly, then let rest covered in a lightly oiled container at room temperature for 2 to 4 hours until puffy, supple, and extensible — never fight cold, tight dough! Never refreeze thawed dough.

  4. D) Never Freeze Raw Poolish Alone

    Always freeze finished portioned dough balls rather than Day 1 liquid poolish — ice crystals damage high-moisture preferment yeast unpredictably.
