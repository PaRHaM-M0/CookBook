Stuffed Crust Pepperoni Pizza with Guanciale Tomato Sauce

Source: Joshua Weissman — Best Pizza Hut Stuffed Crust Recipe

🍕
Yield
2 twelve-inch (30-cm) pizzas (16 slices; 4–6 servings)
⏱️
Prep Time
40 minutes active
🧊
Inactive Time
1 hour 20 minutes
🔥
Bake Time
6–10 mins/pizza (+10m stone recovery)
🕰️
Total Time
About 2 hours 20 minutes
Equipment Needed Phase 1: Mixing & Proofing Dough Phase 2: Thermal Setup & Cheese Freeze Phase 3: Guanciale Tomato Sauce Phase 4: Garlic-Parmigiano Glaze Phase 5: Rolling, Stuffing & Topping Phase 6: Baking, Glazing & Pizza #2 Chef's Notes & Culinary Science

Ingredients

Inactive Time Breakdown: 5 min yeast bloom + 60 min bulk rise + 15–20 min bench rest; overlapping 45–60 min oven preheat and 30–45 min cheese freeze.

Pizza Dough (Yields ~875 g total; two ~438 g balls)
Warm water (95°F / 35°C) 320 g / 320 ml (1⅓ cups)
Instant dry yeast 8 g (2½ tsp)
Unbleached all-purpose flour, plus 20–30 g extra for dusting (optional upgrade: swap 250 g for bread flour for extra chew and rim strength) 500 g (3½ cups)
Granulated sugar 14 g (1 tbsp)
Fine sea salt (or 9 g / 1¾ tsp for a more assertive artisan crust) 6 g (1¼ tsp)
Extra-virgin olive oil, plus extra for oiling bowls 35 g / 38 ml (2½ tbsp)
Guanciale Tomato Sauce (Yields ~375 g total; ~185 g / ¾ cup per pizza)
Guanciale, tough outer rind trimmed off, cut into ¼-inch (6-mm) dice (substitute: unsmoked pancetta) 113 g (4 oz)
Extra-virgin olive oil 54 g / 60 ml (¼ cup)
Garlic, thinly sliced 4 medium cloves (about 16 g)
Crushed tomatoes — high-quality (such as Bianco DiNapoli or Cento) 1 can (411 g / 14.5 oz)
Fresh thyme, tied together with kitchen twine 4 sprigs
Fresh sage, tied into the same herb bundle 3 sprigs
Freshly ground black pepper ½ tsp
Fine sea salt (added at the end after tasting) To taste
Garlic-Parmigiano Crust Glaze (Covers both pizzas)
Salted butter (if using unsalted butter, add 2 g / ⅓ tsp fine sea salt) 75 g (⅓ cup / 5⅓ tbsp)
Garlic, grated into a fine paste on a rasp grater 3 medium cloves (about 12 g)
Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated on a rasp grater 8–10 g (1 heaping tbsp)
Assembly & Toppings (Full 2-pizza batch, with single-pizza breakdown)
Low-moisture whole-milk mozzarella string cheese sticks, unwrapped (7–8 sticks / 200–225 g per pizza) 14–16 sticks (395–450 g / 14–16 oz)
Fine cornmeal or semolina flour, for dusting the peel (1½–2 tbsp per pizza) 25–30 g (3–4 tbsp total)
Low-moisture whole-milk block mozzarella, shredded on the large holes of a box grater (113 g / 1 cup per pizza) 225 g (8 oz / 2 cups total)
Parmigiano-Reggiano, freshly grated (45 g / ½ cup per pizza) 90 g (3.2 oz / 1 cup total)
Natural-casing pepperoni, sliced ⅛ inch (3 mm) thick (70–85 g / ~24 slices per pizza) 140–170 g (5–6 oz / ~48 slices)

Overview: An upgraded home-oven version of classic pizzeria stuffed-crust pizza built from scratch for superior flavor and structural reliability. A supple olive-oil-enriched dough is rolled thin and wrapped around a continuous ring of semi-frozen low-moisture whole-milk mozzarella sticks, eliminating hollow air pockets and preventing rim blowouts under high heat. Instead of raw sweetened tomato sauce, cured guanciale is rendered slowly from a cold pan with sliced garlic, fresh thyme, and sage to create a rich, savory tomato base, and the blistered crust collar is finished off-heat with salted garlic-Parmigiano butter.

Hardware

Equipment Needed

  • Digital kitchen scale
  • Stand mixer fitted with a dough hook
  • Rolling pin
  • 14- to 16-inch (36- to 40-cm) pizza stone or baking steel
  • 14-inch (36-cm) wooden pizza peel (for assembly and launching)
  • Metal turning peel or wide metal spatula (for rotating and retrieval)
  • 3-quart (2.8-L) heavy-bottomed saucepan + small saucepan
  • Kitchen twine, rasp grater (Microplane), box grater, and pastry brush
  • Wire cooling rack, large cutting board, and heavy chef's knife or pizza rocker
Phase 1

Mixing, Kneading & Proofing the Dough

🥣 Steps 1–8 · 5m yeast bloom · 5–6m machine + 1–2m hand knead · 60m bulk rise + 15–20m bench rest

  1. Bloom the Yeast

    Stir 8 g (2½ tsp) of instant yeast into 320 g / 320 ml (1⅓ cups) of 95°F (35°C) water in a liquid measuring cup and let stand for 5 minutes until foamy on the surface.

  2. Whisk the Dry Ingredients

    Combine 500 g (3½ cups) of unbleached all-purpose flour, 14 g (1 tbsp) of sugar, and 6 g (1¼ tsp) of fine sea salt in the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the dough hook, whisking briefly to blend.

  3. Coat with Olive Oil

    Turn the mixer to low speed and pour in 35 g / 38 ml (2½ tbsp) of extra-virgin olive oil, mixing for 15–20 seconds until dispersed evenly through the dry flour.

  4. Hydrate the Flour

    With the mixer still running on low speed, pour the bloomed yeast mixture steadily down the side of the bowl over 30 seconds until a shaggy dough comes together with no dry patches at the bottom.

  5. Knead by Machine

    Increase the mixer speed to medium-low and knead for 5–6 minutes until the dough cleans the sides of the bowl and wraps smoothly around the hook.

  6. Knead by Hand and Shape

    Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured counter, knead with the heels of your hands for 1–2 minutes, and round it into a smooth, taut ball that springs back slowly when pressed with a fingertip.

  7. Bulk Rise

    Place the dough seam-side down in a lightly oiled bowl, turn once to coat the surface, cover tightly with plastic wrap, and proof in a warm spot (75–80°F / 24–27°C) for 60 minutes until doubled in size.

  8. Divide and Bench Rest

    Gently deflate the dough, divide it on a scale into two equal ~438 g portions, shape each into a tight seam-sealed ball, cover with oiled plastic wrap, and let rest at room temperature for 15–20 minutes to relax the gluten.

Phase 2

Oven Thermal Setup & Pre-Freezing the Cheese

🧊 Steps 9–10 · 45–60 min max stone/steel preheat · 30–45 min mozzarella stick freeze

  1. Preheat the Stone or Steel

    Set a rack in the upper-middle third of the oven, place your pizza stone or steel on it, and preheat to your oven's maximum setting (500–550°F / 260–288°C) for 45–60 minutes.

  2. Freeze the Mozzarella Sticks

    Arrange the 14–16 unwrapped mozzarella sticks in a single layer on a parchment-lined plate and freeze for 30–45 minutes until firm to the touch so the cheese melts cleanly in the oven without bursting out of the crust.

Phase 3

Making the Guanciale Tomato Sauce

🍅 Steps 11–16 · Cold-start guanciale render · Thyme & sage simmer · Cool completely before use

  1. Render the Guanciale from Cold

    Spread 113 g (4 oz) of diced guanciale evenly across a cold 3-quart (2.8-L) heavy-bottomed saucepan, set over medium heat, and cook for 3–5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the white fat turns translucent and renders into liquid while the edges turn pale gold.

  2. Sauté the Garlic

    Stir 54 g / 60 ml (¼ cup) of extra-virgin olive oil and the 4 thinly sliced garlic cloves (~16 g) into the rendered fat and cook over medium-low heat for 60–90 seconds until fragrant and pale blonde without browning.

  3. Add Tomatoes and Herbs

    Carefully pour in the 411 g (14.5 oz) can of crushed tomatoes, add the twine-tied bundle of 4 thyme sprigs and 3 sage sprigs along with ½ tsp of black pepper, and stir well to deglaze the bottom of the pan.

  4. Simmer to Thicken

    Simmer uncovered over medium-low heat for 5–8 minutes, stirring occasionally, until watery liquid evaporates and a spoon pulled across the pan leaves a brief trail.

  5. Discard Herbs and Season

    Remove the pan from the heat, lift out and discard the herb bundle, taste the sauce, and add fine sea salt sparingly only if needed to balance the saltiness of the guanciale.

  6. Cool Completely

    Transfer the sauce to a wide bowl and cool to room temperature before assembly so hot sauce does not soften the raw dough on the peel.

Phase 4

Making the Garlic-Parmigiano Glaze

🧈 Steps 17–19 · Low melt with zero browning · 1–2 min off-heat garlic steep

  1. Melt the Butter

    Melt 75 g (⅓ cup) of salted butter in a small saucepan over low heat until liquid with zero browning, then pull the pan off the burner right away.

  2. Infuse the Garlic Off-Heat

    Stir the 3 rasp-grated garlic cloves (~12 g) into the warm butter and let steep off the heat for 1–2 minutes to mellow the raw bite without scorching the garlic.

  3. Whisk in the Cheese

    Stir in 8–10 g (1 heaping tbsp) of finely grated Parmigiano-Reggiano and set the saucepan beside a pastry brush near your cooling rack.

Phase 5

Rolling, Stuffing & Topping the First Pizza

🍕 Steps 20–26 · Roll 14–14.5 in circle · Build directly on dusted peel · Launch within 60–90 seconds

  1. Roll the Dough Ball

    Place one 438 g rested dough ball on a lightly floured counter (keep the second covered) and roll from the center outward with a rolling pin, rotating frequently, into an even 14- to 14.5-inch (36-cm) circle about ⅛ to ¼ inch (3–6 mm) thick.

  2. Transfer Empty Dough to the Peel

    Dust a 14-inch wooden peel generously with 1½–2 tbsp (12–15 g) of fine cornmeal or semolina, drape the bare dough circle onto the peel, and give a short shake to confirm it slides freely before adding any toppings.

  3. Lay the Mozzarella Ring

    Arrange 7–8 semi-frozen mozzarella sticks (200–225 g) end-to-end in a circle set 1 to 1¼ inches (2.5–3 cm) inside the outer edge of the dough, trimming the last stick cleanly so the ends butt together without gaps or overlaps.

  4. Moisten the Seam Line

    Lightly brush water along a ½-inch (1.2-cm) strip of dough directly inside the cheese ring so the folded flap adheres tightly without wetting the underside of the pie.

  5. Fold and Crimp the Rim

    Fold the outer 1-inch edge of dough snugly up and over the mozzarella sticks around the entire circle, pressing out trapped air pockets and pressing your fingertips firmly along the moistened inner strip to lock in a 12-inch (30-cm) stuffed collar.

  6. Verify Slide and Sauce

    Give the peel another quick shake to confirm the stuffed crust still moves freely, then spoon half of the cooled sauce (~185 g / ¾ cup) into the center and spread evenly right up to the inner seam.

  7. Cheese and Top Quickly

    Scatter 113 g (1 cup) of shredded block mozzarella and 45 g (½ cup) of grated Parmigiano-Reggiano evenly over the sauce, arrange ~24 slices (70–85 g) of pepperoni on top, and launch within 60–90 seconds before moisture penetrates the crust.

Phase 6

Baking, Glazing & Second Pizza Workflow

🔥 Steps 27–32 · 6–10 mins at 500–550°F (260–288°C) · 60s wire rack vent · 10m stone recovery

  1. Launch onto the Stone

    Set the tip of the wooden peel near the back edge of the hot stone or steel at a shallow angle and draw the peel back in short, steady strokes to slide the pizza flat onto the center.

  2. Bake and Rotate

    Bake at 500–550°F (260–288°C) for 6–10 minutes, rotating 180 degrees halfway through (around minute 4–5), until the crust collar is puffed and golden brown, the center cheese blisters, and the pepperoni edges crisp and curl.

  3. Vent on a Wire Rack

    Transfer the hot pizza immediately to a wire cooling rack for 60 seconds so steam escapes from the underside rather than turning the base soggy on a solid board.

  4. Brush the Stuffed Rim

    Stir the warm garlic-Parmigiano butter to resuspend the solids and brush generously over the entire hot puffed crust collar.

  5. Rest and Slice

    Let rest 1–2 minutes more so the molten cheese inside the rim sets into a clean stretch rather than spilling out, then move to a cutting board and slice into 8 wedges with a sharp chef's knife or rocker blade.

  6. Reheat the Stone for Pizza #2

    Close the oven and let the stone or steel recover heat for 10 minutes at 500–550°F (260–288°C) before repeating Steps 20–31 with the remaining dough, frozen mozzarella sticks, sauce, toppings, and glaze.

Science

Chef's Notes & Culinary Science

  1. Why Bare Dough Goes onto the Peel First

    A fully stuffed and topped 12-inch pizza weighs over 900 g (2 lb) and has heavy weight concentrated around a soft outer seam. Building directly on a cornmeal-dusted wooden peel eliminates the risk of stretching, tearing, or unsealing the rim mid-transfer.

  2. Why Rolling Beats Hand-Stretching Here

    Unlike Neapolitan cornicione—which relies on expanding gas pockets for rim height—stuffed crust gets its volume from 200+ g of enclosed mozzarella. Degassing with a rolling pin builds a thin, uniform skin without large air bubbles that blow open and leak cheese at 500°F+.

  3. Thermal Timing of Semi-Frozen String Cheese

    Whole-milk low-moisture mozzarella string cheese possesses aligned protein fibers designed for string pull. Freezing the sticks for 30–45 minutes delays core liquefaction long enough for the outer dough collar to set, brown, and seal.

  4. Cold-Pan Rendering & Off-Heat Garlic

    Starting guanciale in a cold pan melts connective fat gently before lean muscle proteins seize. Similarly, steeping rasp-grated garlic in off-heat melted butter extracts oil-soluble aromatics without scorching the sugars that turn burnt garlic bitter.
