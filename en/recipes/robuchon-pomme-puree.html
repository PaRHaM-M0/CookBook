Robuchon Pommes Purée

The legendary dish that earned Joël Robuchon global acclaim across all his Michelin-starred dining rooms: skin-simmered Ratte or Yukon Gold potatoes dried in the pan, emulsified cube-by-cube with chilled butter, whipped airy, and passed through a fine drum sieve.

Ingredients

Only 4 ingredients. Source: Signature Dishes — Joël Robuchon Pommes Purée.

Robuchon Pommes Purée
Ratte, Yukon Gold, or Fingerling potatoes (unpeeled, uniform size)2 lbs (~900 g)
Unsalted butter, well-chilled & cut into small cubes1 lb (~454 g)
Whole milk, brought just to a boil½ to ¾ cup (as needed)
Sea salt (10 g per liter of cooking water + to finish)To taste
Step 1

Simmer Whole in Skins

  1. Scrub potatoes clean — do not peel (skins block waterlogged starch). Cover by at least 1 inch cold water seasoned with 10 g salt per liter, simmer uncovered 20–30 minutes until knife-tender, and drain immediately. Meanwhile bring milk just to a boil and keep hot.

Step 2

Peel Hot, Mill & Steam-Dry

  1. Peel potatoes while still piping hot, pass through a food mill or potato ricer into a heavy-bottomed saucepan, and stir vigorously with a wooden spatula over low heat for 4–5 minutes to evaporate excess moisture.

Step 3

Butter & Hot Milk Emulsion

  1. Beat in cold cubed butter a small handful at a time, stirring vigorously so each addition emulsifies completely before adding the next.

  2. Stream in boiling milk in a thin thread while stirring fast until voluptuous, then whip briskly with a wire whisk to aerate.

Step 4

Fine Drum Sieve & Bain-Marie Hold

  1. Push purée through a fine-mesh drum sieve (tamis) 2 to 3 times for velvet perfection, adjust salt, and serve small rich portions immediately (or hold warm up to 1 hour over a simmering double boiler). Source: Signature Dishes.
