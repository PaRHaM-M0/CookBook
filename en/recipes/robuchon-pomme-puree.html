Robuchon Pommes Purée

Robuchon Pommes Purée — Joël Robuchon famously earned his Michelin stars behind this iconic dish, still served across all his restaurants worldwide. Its secret lies in the legendary 2:1 potato-to-butter ratio, drying the riced potato flesh over low heat, and passing the emulsion through a fine drum sieve until liquid-silk smooth — and because it is extraordinarily rich, a small portion per person goes a long way.

Ingredients

🥔 Robuchon Pommes Purée
Ratte or Yukon Gold potatoes2 lbs (~900 g)
Cold butter, cut into small cubes1 lb (~454 g)
Milk (as needed)½ to ¾ cup
Salt (+ 10 g per liter of cooking water)To taste

Essential equipment: food mill or potato ricer, wooden spatula, balloon whisk, and a fine-mesh drum sieve (tamis).

Phase 1

Simmering the Potatoes Skin-On

⏱️ 20 to 30 minutes — drain the instant they are tender; never let them sit in water

  1. Scrub the potatoes thoroughly, leaving their skins intact. Cook them whole and unpeeled in enough cold water to cover by at least 1 inch, adding 10 g of salt per liter of water.

  2. Simmer gently uncovered over medium heat for 20 to 30 minutes, or until a knife slides in and slips back out effortlessly.

  3. The moment the potatoes are cooked through, lift and drain them immediately. Do not let them sit in the water.

  4. Meanwhile, bring the milk just to a bare simmer in a medium saucepan and set aside.

Phase 2

Peeling, Milling & Stovetop Drying

🥄 Low heat, stirring constantly with a wooden spatula for about 4 to 5 minutes

  1. As soon as the potatoes are just cool enough to handle (while still very hot), peel off the skins and cut into manageable pieces. Discard the peels or save them for another dish.

  2. Pass the hot potatoes through a food mill (or press through a potato ricer) directly into a large, heavy-bottomed saucepan.

  3. Set the pan over low heat and stir vigorously with a wooden spatula to drive off excess moisture and dry the puree out (about 4 to 5 minutes).

Phase 3

Emulsifying Cold Butter First, Then Hot Milk

  1. Begin incorporating three-quarters of the cold cubed butter bit by bit, stirring vigorously until fully absorbed before adding more. Treat this exactly like building a classic beurre blanc emulsion: start with very small additions so a stable emulsion forms.

  2. Drizzle in the hot milk slowly in a thin thread until your desired consistency is reached. Depending on the moisture of the potato variety used, the butter absorbed, and personal preference, you may only need a very small amount of milk. Beat vigorously until every drop is absorbed.

  3. Switch to a whisk and beat briskly to whip air into the puree until light and fluffy.

Emulsion Sequence Cold butter goes in first in small increments, starting tiny to establish the emulsion; hot milk follows afterward in a gradual thread. The original text stresses adding both gradually and notes that final milk volume depends on the potato batch rather than a fixed number.
Phase 4

Fine Drum Sieve Pass, Seasoning & Warm Hold

  1. Push the puree through a fine-mesh drum sieve (tamis) to make it lighter and completely velvet-smooth. You can repeat this pass 2 or 3 times for true restaurant silkiness.

  2. Taste and adjust seasoning. If not serving immediately, place over a bain-marie of barely simmering water, stirring occasionally so it stays silky. Adjust consistency right before service with a splash more hot milk or the remaining cold butter.

  3. If holding ahead of service, the puree keeps warm smoothly in a bain-marie for up to 1 hour.

Source Reminder Because this puree is extraordinarily rich, you only need a modest portion per guest.
