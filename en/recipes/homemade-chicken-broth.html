Easy Homemade Chicken Broth

Momsdish's Game-Changing Golden Broth — zero vegetable peeling required (onion skin gives deep amber color!), fresh or frozen drumsticks straight into the pot, and 3–4 hours of gentle bare-simmer marrow extraction.

Prep Time
5 Minutes
Simmer Time
3 to 4 Hours (Uncovered)
Yield
3 Servings (8 qt Water → 3–4 qt Broth)
Source Rating
4.8 / 5 (741 Votes)
Skill Level
Easy

Ingredients

Per author Natalya Drozhzhin: starts with 8 quarts (~7.5 L) cold filtered water and reduces to 3–4 quarts (~2.8–3.8 L) rich golden broth.

Recipe Card Core
Bone-in Chicken Drumsticks (fresh or frozen; organic ideal)6 drumsticks
Medium Carrots (scrubbed clean, unpeeled)3 medium
Medium Onion (washed, skin kept ON for golden color!)1 medium
Whole Garlic Head (unpeeled, intact)1 whole head
Bay Leaves + Salt3 leaves + 2 tbsp
Cold Filtered WaterFill stockpot (~8 qt)
 Optional Aromatics
Large celery chunks + Fresh dill, parsley, rosemary, or thymeOptional

Nutrition/serving: 282 kcal · 28 g Protein · 10 g Carbs · 14 g Fat · 4852 mg Sodium.

Step 1

5-Minute Zero-Peel Pot Setup

  1. Place chicken drumsticks (fresh, frozen, or a stripped rotisserie chicken carcass), scrubbed unpeeled carrots, skin-on onion, whole garlic head, bay leaves, salt, and optional large celery chunks/herbs into a large stockpot.

Step 2

3 to 4-Hour Bare Simmer (Or Crock-Pot Option)

  1. Fill pot with cold filtered water, bring to a boil, drop heat to very low, and simmer uncovered for 3–4 hours. (Crock-Pot option: cook on Low for 6–8 hours.)

Step 3

Crystal-Clear Paper-Towel Strain

  1. Strain broth through a colander lined with a paper towel for crystal clarity. Discard spent veggies, save and shred the cooked chicken meat for soup!

Step 4

Storage & Glass-Shatter Warning

Never Freeze in Glass Mason Jars!Expanding ice shatters glass jars in the freezer! Store up to 1 week in the fridge, or freeze for several months exclusively in reusable plastic containers or freezer bags.
Notes

Broth vs. Stock & Direct Soup Link

  1. Broth uses more meat (lighter, sippable like tea), whereas Stock uses more bones (richer gelatin body). Put this batch straight to work in our Lemon Chicken Orzo Soup! Source: Momsdish — Natalya Drozhzhin (Official Video).
