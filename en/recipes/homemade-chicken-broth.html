Easy Homemade Chicken Broth

Momsdish's Easy Homemade Chicken Broth — the foundational liquid gold behind great soups, stews, grains, and sauces, soothing enough to sip straight from a warm mug when fighting a cold. What makes this formula unbeatable is pure zero-prep simplicity: nothing gets peeled, chicken can go in fresh or rock-frozen, and 3 to 4 hours of bare simmering extracts deep marrow richness and natural golden color.

Ingredients

Yields 3 full soup bases (per author's notes: starts in an 8-quart / ~7.5 L stockpot filled with water and reduces gently to 3–4 quarts / 2.8–3.8 L rich golden broth).

Golden Rule of Color & Flavor Stop wasting time with a vegetable peeler! Scrub carrots, onion, and the whole garlic bulb clean and toss them straight into the pot skin-on — everything gets strained out anyway, and papery yellow onion skins tint the broth a gorgeous deep gold while bone marrow delivers true body.
Phase 1

Loading the Stockpot (5 Minutes Prep)

Works equally well with fresh or straight-from-freezer chicken — zero peeling

  1. Load Core Ingredients Together

    Place the bone-in drumsticks, whole unpeeled carrots, bay leaves, skin-on whole onion, whole unpeeled garlic head, and salt directly into a large 8-quart stockpot. Fresh or frozen chicken goes straight in as-is.

  2. Optional Herb & Celery Boost

    If desired, tuck in coarsely chunked celery ribs (cut big so they strain out cleanly without turning mushy) plus fresh dill, parsley, rosemary, or thyme.

Rotisserie Chicken Carcass Hack Have a store-bought rotisserie chicken on hand? Pull and shred the meat for tonight's soup, then drop the entire leftover carcass, skin, and bones straight into the pot for roasted depth with zero waste.
Phase 2

Bringing to a Boil & 3–4 Hour Bare Simmer

Lowest bare simmer uncovered — or 6 to 8 hours on Low in a Crock-Pot

  1. Fill with Cold Filtered Water

    Fill the stockpot completely with cold water — preferably filtered so tap chlorine or mineral off-notes never cloud the clean flavor — and bring to a boil over high heat.

  2. Long Gentle Simmer

    The moment it boils, drop the burner to very low and let simmer gently uncovered for 3 to 4 hours so bone marrow collagen and aromatics extract slowly and cleanly.

Hands-Off Slow Cooker (Crock-Pot) Option Prefer leaving the house without an open burner? Load everything into a slow cooker, cover with water, cook on Low for 6 to 8 hours, and strain identically.
Phase 3

Paper-Towel Crystal Strain & Saving the Meat

  1. Double-Layer Clarifying Strain

    Set a colander over a large bowl, line the inside with a single sheet of kitchen paper towel, and pour the broth through — trapping every fine particle and fat speck for jewel-clear golden broth.

  2. Save Every Bit of Chicken Meat

    Discard the spent vegetables whose flavor is now fully in the pot, but do NOT throw away the drumsticks! Pull all tender cooked chicken meat off the bones and save it for soups, quesadillas, or chicken salad.

Phase 4

Refrigerator & Freezer Storage

  1. Refrigerator (Short Term)

    Store strained cooled broth tightly sealed in the refrigerator for up to 1 full week.

  2. Freezer (Long Term)

    Portion cooled broth into lidded plastic deli containers or heavy-duty freezer zip bags and freeze flat for several months of instant soup base.

Critical Glass Explosion Warning Direct warning from Natalya's own kitchen mishap: NEVER freeze broth in glass Mason jars! Liquid expands powerfully as it freezes and shatters glass jars inside your freezer. Use strictly plastic containers or freezer bags.
Appendix

Chicken Broth vs. Chicken Stock, Uses & Source

  1. Chicken Broth vs. Chicken Stock — What's the Difference?

    Per the source, chicken broth simmers a higher proportion of meat alongside bones for a lighter, clean sipping body, whereas chicken stock relies heavily on cartilage-rich bones for thicker gelatin body. Adjust meat-to-bone ratio freely to suit your kitchen.

  2. Best Culinary Uses

    Beyond sipping warm from a mug, use directly as the backbone for Lemon Chicken Orzo Soup (right here in CookBook — using both this broth and its pulled chicken!), Chicken Noodle Soup, Chicken Gizzard & Mushroom Soup, Clam Chowder, risottos, and cooking grains — with total control over salt vs. boxed grocery cartons.
