Five-Cheese Baked Cavatappi Mac and Cheese

With Bonito-Infused Aromatics

Cavatappi is folded into a rich cream-and-milk sauce with five cheeses, then layered and broiled until the top is bubbling and deeply golden. Garlic, thyme, bay, and bonito steep in the dairy first; the solids are strained out before the cheeses go in.

Yield
12–16 side-dish servings
Prep time
20 minutes
Cook time
About 45 minutes
Rest time
15 minutes
Total time
About 1 hour 20 minutes

Ingredients

Ingredient amounts and yield scale; cooking times, temperatures and rack position do not. Adjust the dish and pot sizes or cook in batches.

Pasta
Cavatappi1 lb (454 g)
Kosher salt, for the pasta waterAs needed
Olive oil, for tossing after draining1 tsp
Infused Dairy
Heavy cream3 cups (720 ml)
Whole milk2 c (475 ml)
Garlic cloves, lightly crushed5
Fresh thyme sprigs8
Bay leaves2
Bonito flakes½ cup (15 g)
Dry mustard1 tsp
Freshly ground black pepper½ tsp
Smoked paprika¼ tsp
Cheese
Sharp white cheddar, freshly grated½ lb (227 g)
Whole-milk, low-moisture mozzarella, freshly grated½ lb (227 g)
Young Gouda, rind removed and freshly grated1 lb (454 g)
Mild goat cheese (log), cut into small pieces4 oz (113 g)
Parmesan, finely grated½ cup (50 g)
Kosher saltTo taste
Butter, for greasing the baking dishAs needed

Equipment

  • Large pot for the pasta
  • Heavy-bottomed pot, about 6 qt (5.7 L), for the sauce
  • Cheesecloth and kitchen twine
  • Fine-mesh strainer
  • Large mixing bowl
  • Box grater
  • 9 × 13-inch (23 × 33 cm) baking dish
  • Rimmed baking sheet
Phase 1

Prepare the Oven, Cheese, and Aromatic Sachet

  1. Set up the broiler.

    Position an oven rack about 4 inches (10 cm) below the broiler element. Preheat the broiler. Lightly butter the baking dish and set it on a rimmed baking sheet.

  2. Grate and divide the cheeses.

    Grate the cheddar, mozzarella, and Gouda. Combine them in a large bowl and divide the mixture into two equal portions. Keep the Parmesan separate for the topping. Cut the goat cheese into small pieces.

  3. Make the sachet.

    Place the crushed garlic, thyme, bay leaves, and bonito flakes in the center of a piece of cheesecloth. Tie it securely with kitchen twine, leaving a long end so you can lift it out of the pot. This lets the aromatics flavor the dairy without leaving flakes and herb stems in the finished sauce.

Phase 2

Cook the Pasta

  1. Boil the pasta.

    Bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it generously. Add the cavatappi and cook it 2 minutes less than the package’s al-dente time. If the package says 6 minutes, start tasting at 4 minutes. The pasta should be firm and a little underdone; it will continue cooking in the sauce and under the broiler.

  2. Drain promptly.

    Drain the pasta as soon as it reaches that stage. Toss it with the olive oil just until lightly coated, then set aside. Don’t leave it sitting in hot water.

Phase 3

Infuse the Dairy

  1. Warm the cream and milk.

    In the heavy-bottomed pot, combine the cream, milk, dry mustard, black pepper, and smoked paprika. Warm over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally, until steaming. Do not bring the dairy to a boil.

  2. Steep the aromatics.

    Add the sachet and maintain a very gentle simmer for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally. Keep the heat low; the dairy should steam gently, not bubble vigorously. Remove the sachet and let it drain over the pot, but don’t squeeze it. Discard the solids.

  3. Return the dairy to a gentle simmer.

    Bring the strained mixture back to steaming over low heat. It may reduce slightly, but it should still be fluid enough to form a smooth cheese sauce.

Phase 4

Make the Cheese Sauce

  1. Melt in half of the cheese blend.

    Lower the heat. Add one portion of the cheddar, mozzarella, and Gouda a handful at a time, stirring gently until each addition melts before adding the next.

  2. Add the goat cheese.

    Stir in the goat cheese until it melts into the sauce. It adds a light tang and creaminess that keeps the rich dairy and mild cheeses from tasting flat.

  3. Fold in the pasta.

    Add the drained cavatappi and fold until every piece is coated. Bring the mixture just back to a gentle simmer, then take it off the heat. Taste before adding salt; the cheeses and pasta water already bring saltiness. If the sauce seems too thick, stir in a small splash of warm milk.

Phase 5

Layer and Broil

  1. Build the layers.

    Spoon half of the mac and cheese into the prepared baking dish and spread it evenly. Scatter the reserved cheddar-mozzarella-Gouda mixture over the top. Add the remaining mac and cheese, then finish with an even layer of Parmesan.

  2. Broil until bubbling and golden.

    Broil the dish on the prepared sheet pan until the top is deeply golden and bubbling, about 5–10 minutes. Rotate the pan as needed for even browning. Start checking after 3 minutes; broilers vary, and the top can burn quickly.

  3. Rest before serving.

    Let the mac and cheese stand for 15 minutes. This allows the sauce to settle slightly, so it stays creamy but portions more neatly.

Why These Five Cheeses Work

  • Sharp white cheddar supplies the familiar mac-and-cheese flavor and most of the sharpness. A moderately sharp cheddar gives good flavor without making the sauce too assertive.
  • Gouda contributes a mild, nutty creaminess. Use young Gouda rather than smoked or heavily aged Gouda, which would dominate the other cheeses.
  • Whole-milk, low-moisture mozzarella gives the sauce its stretchy, gooey cheese pull. Keep it cold until grating; it is easier to grate that way. Avoid fresh mozzarella packed in water, which can add too much moisture.
  • Goat cheese brings a gentle tang and soft texture. A mild, fresh chèvre log works best.
  • Parmesan goes on top rather than into the main cheese blend, adding a salty, savory finish and browning under the broiler.

Grate the cheeses from blocks rather than using pre-shredded cheese; it generally melts more smoothly. The three grated cheeses are divided deliberately: one half melts into the sauce, and the other half forms a distinct layer inside the casserole.

Aromatic and Serving Notes

  • The bonito, garlic, thyme, and bay are infused in a tied sachet, then removed before the cheese is added. The bonito contributes savory depth; it should not make the dish taste overtly fishy. Omit it if you want the same garlic-thyme infusion without a seafood note.
  • Cavatappi is useful here because its ridges and curves catch the thick sauce. Elbow macaroni or another ridged pasta can substitute.
  • Serve as a rich holiday side or a main with a crisp green salad. Refrigerate leftovers in a covered container for up to 3 days; reheat gently with a splash of milk.
