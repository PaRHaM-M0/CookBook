Italian Seasoning

Homemade Italian Seasoning — an all-purpose pantry staple for pasta sauces, marinades, soups, and roasted vegetables. Balanced, herbal, and deeply fragrant, with zero skill barrier: simply measure and mix! Making your own costs far less than store-bought jars, lets you control salt and heat to taste, and guarantees a 100% gluten-free blend.

Ingredients

Yield: 8 tablespoons. Equipment needed: 1 airtight spice jar.

🌿 Core Blend
Dried basil2 tbsp
Dried oregano2 tbsp
Dried parsley2 tbsp
Dried rosemary1 tbsp
Dried thyme leaves1 tbsp
Red pepper flakes1 tbsp
Garlic powder1 tsp
➕ Optional Add-Ins (If On Hand)
Dried marjoram1 tsp
Onion powder½ tsp
Ground sage¼ tsp

Source estimated nutrition (per serving): 13 kcal, 2 g carbohydrates, 20 mg sodium, 79 mg potassium, 1 g fiber. Values are estimates and vary slightly by brand.

Method

Just Two Steps

⏱️ Total time: 5 minutes

  1. Combine all dried herbs and spices in a small bowl and stir until evenly distributed.

  2. Transfer to a clean spice jar or airtight container and store in a cool, dark cabinet for up to 6 months. Enjoy!

Source Batch Tip Scale this recipe up effortlessly into larger pantry batches (on the source printable card, simply adjust serving count). Mixing spice blends at home costs a fraction of retail bottles — make a double batch and stock your cabinet.
Notes

Ingredient Breakdown (From Source)

  1. Herbs: Dried basil and oregano form the backbone of Italian seasoning, giving a versatile Mediterranean flavor profile suited to wide-ranging dishes.

  2. Aromatics: Garlic powder, rosemary, and thyme build savory aromatic depth that elevates homemade blends over flat commercial jars.

  3. Spices: Crushed red pepper flakes add gentle warmth; dial them up for extra kick or omit entirely if cooking for mild palates.

  4. Optional Add-Ins: Whenever you have them on hand, round out the blend with 1 tsp dried marjoram, ½ tsp onion powder, or ¼ tsp ground sage.

Best Uses Per the source, this blend shines in soups, stews, pasta sauces, marinades, and roasted vegetables. Inside this cookbook, it pairs directly with our Fettuccine Alfredo recipes, Lemon Chicken Orzo Soup, and Scialatielli with Shrimp.
Finish

Storage & Source

  1. Storage: Keep sealed in a spice jar or airtight container away from heat and sunlight for up to 6 months. Dried herbs remain safe past 6 months, though volatile oils gradually fade.
