Professional Homemade Ricotta

Professional Homemade Ricotta — nothing more than whole milk, lemon juice, and salt, governed entirely by precision temperature and timing. Milk heats strictly to 180°F (83°C), holds at that exact temperature for 40 minutes so curds cleanly separate from the whey, and hangs undisturbed for 1 to 1.5 hours. Start to finish takes roughly 2 hours.

Milk
½ gallon (just over 1.75 L)
Target Temp
Exactly 180°F (83°C)
Thermal Hold
Exactly 40 minutes
Hanging Drain
1 to 1.5 hours
Total Time
About 2 hours
Skill Level
Medium
Heat & Coagulate Initial Drain Hanging the Curds Salt & Finish

Ingredients & Equipment

Only 3 ingredients — mastery here comes entirely from thermal control and patience, not ingredient count.

Ingredients
Whole milk½ gallon (just over 1.75 L)
Fresh lemon juice¼ cup (60 ml)
SaltTo taste
Required Equipment
Medium heavy-bottomed saucepan1
Instant-read digital thermometer1
Fine-mesh sieve1
CheeseclothGenerous sheet
Kitchen twineExtra-long piece
Slotted spoon or ladle1
Deep catch bowlFor collecting whey
Phases 1–3

Heating the Milk, Adding Acid & Separating Curds

Monitor temperature closely with an instant-read thermometer; magic number: 180°F (83°C)

  1. Warming the Milk

    Pour the milk into a medium pot and warm it gently until it reaches an exact internal temperature of 180°F (83°C), tracking closely with an instant-read thermometer.

  2. Adding the Coagulant

    As soon as the milk hits 180°F, gently stir in ¼ cup lemon juice.

  3. 40-Minute Thermal Hold & Separation

    Adjust the burner to maintain that exact 180°F (83°C) hold for strictly 40 minutes, stirring occasionally. Holding at temperature allows clean, tender curds to separate fully from the pale yellow whey.

Heart of the Formula Two numbers here are completely non-negotiable: hitting exactly 180°F and holding steady at 180°F for a full 40 minutes. Keep your thermometer clipped or ready from minute one rather than scrambling once the milk steams.
Phase 4

Initial Gravity Drain

Natural gravity drain: 5 minutes

  1. Turn off the burner and allow the pot to settle and cool slightly.

  2. While cooling briefly, line a fine-mesh sieve generously with cheesecloth and set it over a large catch bowl.

  3. Carefully ladle the curds and whey into the lined sieve and let drain undisturbed by gravity for 5 minutes.

Phase 5

Hanging the Curd Bundle

Undisturbed drip: 1 to 1.5 hours

  1. Gather all four corners of the cheesecloth up together and twist snug around the curds.

  2. Tie the neck securely with kitchen twine, making sure to leave a long trailing tail of twine.

  3. Tie that extra length of twine around an upper cabinet knob (or wooden spoon suspended over a deep pot) so the bundle hangs freely, positioning your catch bowl directly underneath.

  4. Let drip completely undisturbed without squeezing for 1 to 1.5 hours.

Setup Tip Cut your twine extra-long right from the start — that extra tail is your suspension cord for the cabinet handle — and place the catch bowl underneath before tying off.
Phase 6

Breaking the Curds & Final Seasoning

  1. Once hanging completes (total elapsed time roughly 2 hours start to finish), untie the bundle and transfer the fresh ricotta into a clean mixing bowl.

  2. Use a spoon or clean fingers to open a well in the center and gently break up the curd mass.

  3. Add a pinch of salt to taste, fold gently until evenly seasoned throughout, and adjust to taste.

Final Result Sweet, cloud-soft homemade ricotta whose tender curd structure comes strictly from the 40-minute 180°F hold and unforced 1–1.5 hour hang — salted cleanly right at the end.
