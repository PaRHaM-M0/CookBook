Harira Soup

Harira — Morocco's iconic, deeply aromatic tomato-based soup simmered with chickpeas and lentils, boldly perfumed with ginger, black pepper, cinnamon, turmeric, and generous bunches of fresh flat-leaf parsley and cilantro. Beloved across Morocco — especially at sunset throughout Ramadan to break the fast — it finishes with Tedouira (a silky flour-and-water liaison) for a velvety, cream-smooth body.

Harira stands on two structural pillars: a spiced tomato-and-meat broth simmered right from the start with peeled chickpeas, followed midway by lentils, tomato paste, celery, and herbs. At the finish, traditional Moroccan cooks follow either the Rice Path or the Broken Vermicelli Path — each carrying distinct cooking times, both preserved side-by-side below alongside exact pressure-cooker and conventional Dutch-oven schedules.

Golden Rule Soak dried chickpeas overnight AND slip off their papery skins the next morning. Peeling synchronizes chickpea tenderness with cubed meat and keeps every spoonful silky clean without loose hulls.
Step 1

Advance Preparation Before Lighting Heat

🧺 Chickpeas: soak the night before — Everything else: prep cleanly before searing

  1. Chickpeas — Soaking & Slipping Skins

    Soak dried chickpeas in plenty of water overnight. Next day, drain and slip off the skins — either pinching individual chickpeas between thumb and forefinger or rubbing the whole batch vigorously inside a clean kitchen towel so skins loosen right off.

  2. Sorting the Lentils

    Pick carefully through the dried green or brown lentils to remove any tiny stones or debris; set aside.

  3. Pureeing Fresh Tomatoes

    Simmer or blanch the ripe tomatoes briefly, then pass through a food mill or food processor/strainer into a smooth puree, discarding skins and seeds.

  4. Grating the Onion

    Grate the large onion on a box grater or blitz in a food processor until reduced to a thick pulp paste.

  5. Chopping Fresh Herbs & Celery

    Finely chop the celery stalk (including its tender inner leaves) alongside the flat-leaf parsley and cilantro.

Step 2

Building the Meat, Bone & Chickpea Base

🍲 6-liter+ pressure cooker — OR standard heavy Dutch oven with extended gentle simmer

  1. Browning Meat & Locking the Aromatic Base

    In a 6-liter (or larger) pressure cooker or heavy soup pot, brown the cubed meat in hot oil over medium heat. Add the marrow bones (if using), peeled chickpeas, fresh tomato puree, grated onion pulp, all spices (salt, ginger, black pepper, cinnamon, turmeric), smen / ghee (if using), and 3 cups (710 ml) water. Bring to a boil, seal the pressure lid, and cook at medium pressure for 25 minutes — OR simmer gently covered in a regular pot for 50 to 60 minutes.

  2. Adding Lentils, Tomato Paste, Celery & Fresh Herbs

    Open carefully and stir in the picked lentils, diluted tomato paste mixture, chopped celery, chopped parsley and cilantro, plus 8.5 cups (2 liters) water. Bring back to a boil, cover, and proceed directly to either Step 3 (Rice) OR Step 4 (Vermicelli).

Step 3

Option A — Finishing with Rice (First Path)

🍚 Follow ONLY this path if finishing your Harira with rice

  1. Cook the soup under medium pressure for 30 minutes (or simmer gently in a standard pot for 60 minutes).

  2. Stir in the 3 tablespoons uncooked rice and cook under pressure for 15 minutes more (or simmer gently for 30 minutes until tender).

Step 4

Option B — Finishing with Broken Vermicelli (Second Path)

🍜 Follow ONLY this path instead of Step 3 if finishing with broken vermicelli

  1. Cook the lentil-and-herb soup under pressure for 45 minutes (or simmer gently in a standard pot for 90 minutes).

  2. Stir in the 3 tablespoons broken fine vermicelli and simmer uncovered for a few minutes just until the strands turn tender.

Important Path Note Although the master ingredient table lists 3 tablespoons for rice OR vermicelli, traditional Harira uses strictly one starch path per batch rather than combining both unless you deliberately choose an extra-thick soup.
Step 5

Velvety Tedouira Liaison, Seasoning & Service

🥄 Target final texture: smooth, pouring-cream silkiness

  1. Taste the simmering broth and adjust salt and black pepper as needed.

  2. Thickening Slowly with Tedouira

    Stream the smooth flour-and-water slurry (Tedouira) into the simmering pot slowly and gradually while stirring constantly until the soup reaches a glossy, velvety, cream-like body.

  3. Simmer gently for 5 to 10 minutes more, stirring occasionally to cook out raw flour completely while skimming off any surface foam that rises.

  4. Pull off the heat and ladle into deep warm bowls, serving alongside fresh lemon wedges and chopped cilantro.

Tedouira Slurry Tip Never dump the flour-water mixture in all at once! Pouring slowly in a thin stream while stirring constantly prevents flour dumplings and builds Harira's signature satin sheen over the final 5–10 minutes.
