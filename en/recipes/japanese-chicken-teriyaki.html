Japanese Chicken Teriyaki

Pan-Seared Chicken Thighs with a Glossy Homemade Glaze

Boneless, skin-on chicken thighs are seared until browned, then coated in a classic soy, sake, and mirin glaze. The sauce thickens by reduction, so there is no need for cornstarch or bottled teriyaki sauce.

Japanese Chicken Teriyaki

Phase 1

Prepare

  1. Mix the sauce.

    In a small bowl, whisk together the soy sauce, sake, mirin, and sugar until the sugar begins to dissolve. Set aside near the stove.

  2. Prepare the chicken.

    Pat the thighs very dry with paper towels. Trim off any loose flaps of fat. On the flesh side, make a few shallow cuts through the thicker parts of each thigh, without cutting through the skin. This helps the pieces cook evenly and lie flat.

  3. Season lightly.

    The soy sauce seasons the chicken as it glazes, so use only a small pinch of salt, or none if your soy sauce is salty.

Phase 2

Sear the Chicken

  1. Heat the pan.

    Set a large 12-inch skillet over medium heat. Add the neutral oil and swirl to coat the pan. The oil should shimmer but not smoke.

  2. Sear skin-side down.

    Lay the thighs in the pan skin-side down, leaving space between them. Press each thigh gently with a spatula for the first few seconds so the skin makes even contact with the pan. Cook undisturbed for 6–8 minutes, adjusting the heat if the skin is browning too quickly.

  3. Check the skin.

    The skin should be deeply golden and much of its fat should have rendered. If it sticks, give it another 30–60 seconds; properly browned skin will release more easily.

  4. Turn and cook through.

    Flip the thighs and cook the flesh side for 3–5 minutes, or until the thickest part reaches 165°F (74°C). Check with an instant-read thermometer inserted from the side. If the thighs are especially thick, lower the heat and continue cooking until they reach temperature. USDA poultry-temperature guidance

  5. Set the chicken aside.

    Transfer the thighs to a plate, skin-side up. Pour off excess rendered fat, leaving about 1 teaspoon in the skillet along with the browned bits.

Phase 3

Reduce the Sauce and Glaze

  1. Start the glaze.

    Pour the prepared sauce into the skillet. Bring it to a boil over medium heat, scraping up the browned bits from the pan.

  2. Reduce gently.

    Lower the heat to maintain a steady simmer. Cook for 4–7 minutes, stirring and watching closely, until the sauce is glossy and lightly syrupy. It should coat a spoon but still flow; it will thicken further as it cools. Do not reduce it to a sticky paste in the pan.

  3. Glaze the chicken.

    Return the thighs to the skillet. Turn them briefly in the sauce, then spoon the glaze over the tops. For a glossier coating, turn the skin side into the sauce for the final 20–30 seconds. For crisper skin, keep the skin facing up and spoon sauce over it instead.

  4. Rest and slice.

    Transfer the chicken to a board and rest for 3 minutes. Slice crosswise and arrange over steamed rice. Spoon the remaining glaze over the chicken and finish with scallions or sesame seeds, if using.

Cook’s Notes

  • Use Japanese soy sauce, not dark soy sauce. Chinese dark soy is usually more concentrated in color and has a different flavor; it can overpower this sauce.
  • Use hon-mirin if available. If using a sweetened mirin-style seasoning (aji-mirin), reduce the sugar to 1 tablespoon and taste the sauce before reducing.
  • No cornstarch is needed. The sauce should become glossy as it reduces. If it gets too thick, stir in 1 tablespoon of water.
  • The classic sauce base is soy, sake, and mirin with sugar. Garlic and ginger can be tasty, but they change this into a more heavily seasoned variation rather than the clean, classic-style glaze.
  • Refrigerate leftovers promptly and use within 3 days.

Joshua Weissman’s 3-Ingredient Chicken Teriyaki

Phase 1

Render the Chicken Skin

  1. Separate the skin.

    Remove the skin from the chicken thighs and set it aside. Pat the thighs dry with paper towels.

  2. Render the skin.

    Put the chicken skin in a large nonstick skillet over medium heat. Cook, turning as needed, until it releases about 1–2 tablespoons of fat and becomes crisp. Remove and discard the skin, leaving the rendered fat in the pan.

Phase 2

Brown the Chicken

  1. Sear the thighs.

    Raise the heat to medium-high. Add the thighs to the rendered fat in a single layer; work in batches if needed. Sear for 2–3 minutes per side, until golden. They do not need to be cooked through yet.

  2. Cut the chicken.

    Transfer the thighs to a board and turn off the heat. Cut them into bite-size pieces, then return them to the skillet.

Phase 3

Glaze and Finish

  1. Add the sauce ingredients.

    Pour in the dark soy sauce and honey. Stir to coat the chicken and combine the sauce.

  2. Reduce to a glaze.

    Turn the heat to medium-high and bring the sauce to a boil. Lower the heat to maintain a steady simmer. Cook, stirring often, until the sauce thickens and coats the chicken, about 10–15 minutes. Don’t let the honey-sweetened sauce reduce until sticky in the pan; it will thicken further as it cools.

  3. Check doneness and serve.

    Check the thickest chicken pieces with an instant-read thermometer; the internal temperature must reach 165°F (74°C). Serve hot over plain rice, with optional scallions or sesame seeds. USDA poultry-temperature guidance

Cook’s Notes

  • This version uses dark soy sauce, as Joshua’s recipe specifies. It creates a darker, more intense glaze than regular Japanese soy sauce.
  • The sauce is deliberately simple and bold: serve it with plain rice rather than salting the rice.
  • Joshua’s page lists 5 minutes of prep and 15 minutes of cooking. The actual time can vary with the pan and chicken size; use the thermometer, not the clock alone, to confirm the chicken is cooked through.
