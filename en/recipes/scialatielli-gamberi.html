Scialatielli Aglio, Olio, Peperoncino e Gamberi

Scialatielli with Garlic, Olive Oil, Chili & Shrimp — a coastal Southern Italian twist on timeless aglio, olio e peperoncino, equally at home for dinner with friends, midnight pasta cravings, or Sunday family lunch. Scialatielli is the signature fresh pasta of the Amalfi Coast built specifically to hug seafood sauces, perfumed at the very end with wild fennel fronds.

Ingredients

Serves 4. Note: Italian q.b. = as needed / to taste.

🍝 Scialatielli with Shrimp
Fresh egg/milk pasta (Scialatielli)400 g
Shrimp (red shrimp preferred)400 g
Fresh hot red chili pepper1 whole
Garlic cloves2 cloves
Fresh wild fennel fronds (Finocchietto selvatico)1 small bunch
Cognac1 small shot glass (bicchierino)
Extra-virgin olive oilAs needed (q.b.)
SaltTo taste (q.b.)
🏠 Homemade Scialatielli Dough (Optional)
Durum wheat semolina flour400 g
Whole milk170 ml
Extra-virgin olive oil1 tbsp
Finely grated Pecorino cheese30 g
Egg1 whole

Historical note from source: Scialatielli was invented in Amalfi in 1978 by chef Enrico Cosentino — cut thicker, shorter, and slightly more rustic-rectangular than fettuccine or tagliatelle. The author jokes that tampering with sacred household spaghetti aglio e olio risked domestic tragedy, until sweet red shrimp won everyone over completely.

Phase 1

Cleaning the Shrimp & Building the Cognac Base

  1. Peel the shrimp, pull out the dark intestinal thread (filamento nero), rinse briefly, and pat thoroughly dry.

  2. Pour a generous glug of extra-virgin olive oil into a wide sauté pan — large enough to toss all 400 g of pasta comfortably later.

  3. Peel the whole garlic cloves and set them gently into the oil.

  4. Sauté over medium heat for a few minutes until fragrant and golden, then add the dry shrimp.

  5. Sear just until the shrimp blush pink, immediately splash in the Cognac and let the alcohol flash off (sfumare), add the sliced hot chili pepper, adjust salt, and turn off the heat.

Critical Sequence Cognac goes in right as the shrimp take color and evaporates fully; chili and salt follow right at the end of the sear, and the burner shuts off immediately so the shrimp stay tender awaiting the pasta.
Phase 2

Boiling Scialatielli & Direct Pan Emulsion

🍝 Fresh pasta cooks in just minutes — lift straight from pot to skillet while dripping wet

  1. Drop the scialatielli into a large pot of boiling salted water; fresh pasta takes only a few minutes.

  2. The instant it hits al dente, lift the strands directly from the pot with a pasta fork (forchettone) into the shrimp skillet without draining dry in a colander — bringing starchy cooking water straight into the pan to build a glossy emulsion.

  3. Toss briskly over lively heat (saltare) for a minute or two, scatter in the picked wild fennel fronds, and plate immediately, piping hot.

Cream-Free Glaze Secret All body in this sauce comes from starchy pasta water emulsified with olive oil, Cognac reduction, and shrimp juices — never drain fresh scialatielli dry in a colander.
Special Section

Making Scratch Scialatielli at Home (From Source)

🏠 Knead: ~10 mins — Fridge rest: min 30 mins — Boil fresh strands: ~5 mins

  1. Mound the semolina flour in a mixing bowl, add 1 lightly beaten egg, fork together, and fold in the finely grated Pecorino.

  2. Stream the milk in gradually in a thin thread (a filo) while beginning to knead by hand. Work in the tablespoon of olive oil and turn out onto a lightly floured counter.

  3. Knead steadily for about 10 minutes until smooth, supple, and elastic. Wrap tightly in plastic wrap and rest in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes to firm up.

  4. Unwrap on a floured work surface, divide in half, and roll out with a rolling pin to a thickness of 5 mm.

  5. Loosely fold the outer edges inward toward the center before cutting, then slice crosswise with a straight sharp knife into 5 mm (½ cm) strips.

  6. Dust the cut scialatielli generously with extra semolina and spread apart so strands never stick together before boiling in salted water for roughly 5 minutes.
