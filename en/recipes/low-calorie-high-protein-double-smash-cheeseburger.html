Low-Calorie, High-Protein Double Smash Cheeseburger

Two thin patties of 96%-lean beef make each burger. A tangy Sriracha-mustard sauce, American cheese, dill pickles, and thinly sliced onion add flavor without piling on extra toppings. View the source recipe.

Ingredients (Makes 4 Burgers)

Each burger uses two 3-ounce (85 g) patties, for eight thin patties total.

Sriracha Sauce
Light mayonnaise1 tbsp (15 g)
Yellow mustard1 tbsp (15 g)
Sriracha2 tbsp (30 g)
Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepperTo taste
Burgers
96%-lean ground beef1½ lb (680 g)
Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepperAs needed
American cheese4 slices
Burger buns, ideally about 160 calories each4
Large dill pickles2
Sweet or yellow onion½
Cold water and ice, for rinsing the onion (optional)As needed

Overview: This four-burger formula keeps the stack focused: two very thin patties made from 96%-lean beef, one slice of American cheese between them, long dill-pickle planks, crisp thin onion, and a tangy Sriracha-mustard sauce on both cut sides of a dry-toasted bun. Prepare every topping before the beef reaches the hot pan; the thin patties cook quickly.

Hardware

Equipment

  • Large cast-iron skillet or flat-top griddle
  • Stiff, wide spatula or burger press
  • Small squares of parchment paper
  • Digital kitchen scale
  • Mandoline with hand guard, or a sharp knife
  • Instant-read thermometer, if available
Steps 1–4

Sauce, Toppings & Cold Beef Portions

⏱️ Prep 15 minutes · Complete before the pan is hot

  1. Mix the Sauce

    In a small bowl, whisk together the 1 tbsp (15 g) light mayonnaise, 1 tbsp (15 g) yellow mustard, and 2 tbsp (30 g) Sriracha. Season with salt and pepper. Set aside while you prepare the toppings and beef.

  2. Slice and Rinse the Onion

    Cut the ½ onion as thinly as possible while keeping the slices intact. A mandoline makes even rings, but a sharp knife works. Rinse briefly in cold water, then drain thoroughly. This takes the edge off a sharp yellow onion while keeping it crisp.

  3. Slice the Pickles

    Cut the 2 large dill pickles lengthwise into thin planks, just under ¼ inch (6 mm) thick. Long slices cover more of the bun than round coins.

  4. Portion the Beef

    Weigh the 1½ lb (680 g) 96%-lean ground beef into eight 3-ounce (85 g) portions. Gently roll each into a ball without packing it tightly. Keep the portions cold until the pan is ready.

Steps 5–10

Dry-Toast, Smash, Sear & Cheese-Stack

🔥 Medium heat for buns → high heat for beef · Cook 10–15 minutes in roomy batches

  1. Toast the Buns

    Heat the skillet or griddle over medium heat. Place the 4 buns cut-side down on the dry surface—no butter or oil—and toast until golden, about 1–2 minutes. Turn or reposition them as needed to toast the cut surfaces evenly. Set aside.

  2. Heat the Pan for the Patties

    Increase the heat to high. The surface should be hot enough that the beef sizzles as soon as it touches the pan. Cook in batches that leave room to press and flip the patties; a crowded pan is harder to work with.

  3. Smash the First Two Patties

    Place two beef balls on the hot surface. Immediately set parchment over each one and press firmly with the spatula until each patty is thin and about ¼ inch (6 mm) wider than the bun. Remove the parchment. Press each patty only once; repeated pressing can squeeze out moisture.

  4. Season and Sear

    Season the exposed side evenly with kosher salt and black pepper. Cook for about 1–2 minutes, until the patties are partly cooked and the edges begin to brown. Because the beef is very lean, don't wait for a thick, deeply browned crust if the patties are already close to done.

  5. Flip and Finish Cooking

    Scrape firmly under each patty so its browned surface releases cleanly, then flip. Cook for about 1 minute more, or until cooked through. Ground beef should reach 160°F (71°C) for food safety; for very thin patties, insert a thin-tip thermometer horizontally through the edge if it can give a reliable reading. See the USDA safe-temperature chart.

  6. Add the Cheese and Stack

    Place one slice of American cheese on one patty. Once it begins to soften, place the plain patty on top of the cheese-topped patty. The cheese will sit between the two patties. Transfer the stack to a warm plate and repeat with the remaining beef.

Steps 11–12

Sauce the Buns, Assemble & Serve

  1. Sauce the Buns

    Spread about 1 teaspoon of sauce on each cut side of each bun. The sauce recipe makes more than a very thin coating requires, so add more to taste.

  2. Assemble and Serve

    Cover the bottom bun with pickle planks, add a loose nest of rinsed onion, and set the double-patty stack on top. Add the top bun and serve immediately.

Cook's Notes

Keep the Formula on Target

  1. Use the Specified Lean Beef

    Use the specified lean beef if you want to stay close to the nutrition estimate. A fattier blend will be juicier but will change the calories and fat.

  2. Have Everything Ready Before Cooking

    These patties are thin and cook quickly; mix the sauce, slice the toppings, and toast the buns first.

  3. Don't Add Fat to the Skillet

    The recipe is designed around dry-toasting the buns and cooking the patties directly on the hot surface.

  4. Timing Note

    The source recipe lists shorter prep and cook times, but the estimates above allow for slicing toppings and cooking in batches on a typical home skillet.
