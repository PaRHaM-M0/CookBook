Korean Fried Chicken

Yangnyeom Chicken — legendary double-fried chicken engineered with pure potato starch for an ultra-light glass-like crunch and tossed fast in a glossy sweet, salty, spicy Gochujang glaze.

Golden RulePure Potato Starch (never plain flour) plus a mandatory cool-down rest between two separate fries is the entire secret behind crusts that stay audible even under sticky sauce.
Step 1

Prep & Ginger-Wine Marinade

  1. Cut the whole chicken into relatively small bone-in pieces and place in a glass bowl.

  2. Add 2–3 tbsp rice wine, 1 tsp grated fresh ginger, salt, and freshly cracked black pepper. Massage thoroughly by hand and let rest so the aromatics penetrate the meat.

Step 2

Packing the Potato Starch Crust

  1. Spread 2–3 cups potato starch in a wide rimmed tray. Drop marinated chicken pieces directly in and press and pack firmly by hand so zero damp spots remain and a thick dry shell forms. Shake off loose excess right before frying.

Step 3

Two-Stage Deep Frying

  1. First Fry — 160 to 170°C (Cook the Core)

    Fry pieces at 160–170°C until the meat cooks through (crust will still look pale). Transfer to a wire cooling rack.

  2. Mandatory Venting Rest — 10 to 15 Minutes

    Let chicken cool completely for 10–15 minutes on the wire rack so internal steam migrates out and evaporates from the starch shell.

  3. Second Fry — Higher Heat (Shatter Crunch)

    Raise oil temperature slightly higher than the first fry and re-fry cooled chicken until deep golden-amber and fiercely crunchy.

Step 4

Warming the Yangnyeom Glaze

  1. Combine honey, ketchup, Gochujang, soy sauce, grated garlic, and sesame oil in a small saucepan over low heat. Stir continuously until glossy, smooth, and sticky — do not boil or scorch, just warm and unify.

Special Feature

Authentic Scratch Gochujang (Korean Master Protocol)

Verified Korean Official SourcesTranslated directly from the Seoul Metropolitan Government's Seoul Agricultural Technology Center (서울시농업기술센터) class taught by 4th-generation traditional master Kim Bok-in (김복인 명인) (Seoul OpenGov), cross-referenced with the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture (한국민족문화대백과사전) (AKS Entry).

  1. 10-Day Glutinous Rice Fermentation

    Soak 1 kg glutinous rice in water to ferment for 7 to 10 days depending on ambient warmth until translucent, then drain.

  2. Malt Sieving & Steaming

    Sieve 500 g barley malt powder and reserve ~1 cup of the finest powder. Mill the fermented rice, knead with water gradually, spread thinly in a steamer lined lightly with sugar and cheesecloth, steam thoroughly, and beat vigorously with a wooden paddle while hot with the reserved malt powder.

  3. Mejugaru First, Chili Second, Salt Last

    Mix in 500 g Mejugaru first with strained malt water from the 4 L water budget (adding Mejugaru first tames its sharp aroma). Beat in 1.5 kg fine Gochujang chili powder gradually until spoon-clinging thick, then season with 500 g sea salt.

  4. 40–50 Day Clay Crock Aging

    Pack into an earthenware crock (Onggi / 항아리) and mature 40 to 50 days for deep umami. Use 2 tbsp of finished paste in the Yangnyeom glaze above.

Step 6

Lightning Glaze & Garnish

  1. Drop piping-hot second-fried chicken into a large mixing bowl, pour warm glaze over top, toss rapidly with tongs so every crevice glistens without softening, plate immediately, and shower with toasted sesame seeds and sliced green scallions.

Appendix

Smart Pantry Substitutions (When Shopping Locally)

  1. Rice Wine Substitute

    Mix 1 tbsp apple cider (or white) vinegar + 1 tbsp water + ½ tsp sugar.

  2. Quick Mock Gochujang (5-Minute Emergency Sub)

    Sauté 2 tbsp tomato paste in a drop of oil for 30s to remove rawness, then whisk with 1 tbsp Sriracha (or hot sauce) + 1 tsp soy sauce + 1 tbsp honey/sugar + ½ tsp garlic powder.

  3. Potato Starch Substitute

    Use white Cornstarch (squeaky white powder — never yellow cornmeal!).
