Five-Spice Cranberry Sauce

This is a smooth cranberry sauce, not a whole-berry relish: cranberries simmer with orange juice, champagne, cognac, and warm spices, then the sauce is blended and strained. Joshua Weissman’s recipe allows fresh or frozen cranberries.

Serves
6
Prep
10 minutes
Cook
20 minutes
Total
30 minutes

Ingredients

Sauce
Cognac¼ cup (60 ml)
Ground cardamom2 tsp (8 g)
Chinese five-spice powder1 tsp (4 g)
Fresh or frozen cranberries1 lb (450 g)
Granulated sugar1½ cups (300 g)
Champagne1 cup (240 ml)
Orange juice1 cup (240 ml)
Phase 1

Reduce the Cognac with the Spices

  1. Heat the cognac.

    Set a medium saucepan over medium heat. Add the cognac and bring it to a simmer.

  2. Add the spices.

    Stir in the cardamom and Chinese five-spice. Simmer until the cognac has reduced by about half—roughly 2 tablespoons remain. Keep it at a controlled simmer; do not ignite or flambé it.

Phase 2

Cook the Cranberries

  1. Combine.

    Add the cranberries, sugar, champagne, and orange juice. Stir to combine.

  2. Simmer.

    Bring the mixture to a boil, then reduce the heat to maintain a steady simmer. Cook for 10–15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until many of the cranberries have burst and the mixture looks thick and jammy. It will thicken further as it cools.

Phase 3

Blend and Strain

  1. Blend.

    Remove the saucepan from the heat and let the mixture stop bubbling. Carefully blend until smooth. If using a countertop blender, work in batches and vent the lid; hot liquid expands. Alternatively, use an immersion blender in the saucepan.

  2. Strain.

    Pour the sauce through a fine-mesh strainer into a bowl, pressing it through with a spatula. Discard the solids left in the strainer.

  3. Cool and store.

    Press plastic wrap directly onto the surface of the sauce to prevent a skin from forming. Cool to room temperature, then serve or refrigerate until needed.

Cranberry Note

  • Frozen cranberries can go straight into the saucepan; thawing is not necessary.

Serving Ideas

  • Serve as a sweet-tart sauce with roast turkey, chicken, or meat.
  • Spread in a turkey or chicken sandwich, or serve alongside soft cheeses and a cheese board.
  • Serve with festive meals and roasted sides, or spoon a little over plain yogurt or creamy desserts.
  • This sauce contains champagne and cognac; cooking does not guarantee that all the alcohol has evaporated.
