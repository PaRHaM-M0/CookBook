Sourdough Bread

Artisan Bakery-Style Crusty Bread (Phase -10 to Phase 100) — the zero-guesswork blueprint for shattering European-bakery crust and open honeycomb crumb right at home. Forget measuring cups completely and turn on your digital scale: nothing more than flour, water, salt, and a tiny 1 gram whisper of yeast.

Ingredients

Makes strictly 1 artisan boule. Weigh every single gram.

🍞 The Dough (4 Pure Ingredients)
High-protein bread flour400 g
Water, room temperature (~25°C)300 g (75%)
Fine sea salt (non-iodized preferred)8 g (2%)
Instant yeastStrictly 1 g (~¼ tsp)

Why only 1 gram of yeast? Because slow 24–72 hour cold fermentation gives organic acids full time to mature — heavy yeast overdoses turn dough boozy, slack, and sour.

Start

Quick Answers Before Mixing

  1. What Does 75% Hydration Mean?

    Water weighs 75% of flour weight (300 ÷ 400 = 0.75) — the sweet spot where dough stays manageable by hand while opening dramatic glossy air pockets inside the loaf.

  2. Should I Add Olive Oil or Sugar?

    Categorically NO! True European artisan loaves rely purely on flour, water, salt, and fermentation — adding fat softens the exterior and robs you of that shattering blistered crust.

  3. Can I Freeze Raw Leftover Dough?

    Avoid freezing raw bread dough (ice crystals weaken gluten and yeast); instead, slice and freeze baked bread — toasted straight from frozen, slices taste fresh from the oven.

Phase -10

Mindset & Essential Gear

  1. Gather a large mixing bowl, an accurate gram scale, high-heat parchment paper, and a heavy lidded cast-iron Dutch oven (or covered enamel/clay pot rated safely to 250°C / 500°F).

Phase 1

Rough Mix & Initial Hydration (Autolyse)

⏱️ Rest: 30 minutes at room temperature

  1. Set your mixing bowl on the scale, tare to zero, and pour in 300 g room-temperature water.

  2. Sprinkle in 1 g instant yeast and swirl briefly.

  3. Add 400 g bread flour.

  4. Mix cleanly with one hand — zero kneading needed! Just squeeze and turn until zero dry flour remains, leaving a shaggy, sticky, ugly mass.

  5. Cover with plastic wrap or a damp towel and rest 30 minutes at room temperature (Autolyse), letting flour hydrate deeply and gluten chains align effortlessly on their own.

Phase 2

Incorporating Salt & Ambient Kickoff

⏱️ Post-salt rest: 30 minutes more

  1. Sprinkle 8 g salt evenly across the dough, dip your hand in water so nothing sticks, and dimple/squeeze the salt thoroughly into the mass until dissolved.

  2. Re-cover and rest another 30 minutes.

Phase 3

Building Strength via Stretch & Fold

⏱️ 3 sets over 1.5 hours (every 30 mins) + 1–2h untouched rest to +30% volume

  1. Instead of bench-kneading, build a strong gas-trapping gluten web right in the bowl: wet your hand, grab the far edge of dough, stretch gently upward without tearing, and fold across the center.

  2. Quarter-turn the bowl 90° and repeat along the right, bottom, and left edges — 4 folds equal one complete Stretch & Fold set.

  3. Cover and rest 30 minutes.

  4. Perform Set #2 of Stretch & Fold; cover and rest 30 minutes.

  5. Perform Set #3 of Stretch & Fold (3 total sets across 90 minutes) — feel how silky, billowy, and cohesive the dough has already turned!

  6. Cover tightly and leave untouched at room temperature for 1 to 2 hours until noticeably puffed by roughly +30% volume.

Phase 4

Surface-Tension Shaping Before Cold Retard

  1. Dust your work counter with only the tiniest whisper of flour.

  2. Ease the puffy dough gently out onto the counter without popping trapped gas pockets.

  3. Coax gently into a loose square, fold all four corners snugly into the center, and flip seam-side down.

  4. Cup both hands behind the far edge and drag the boule toward you across the counter in smooth circular passes to pull tight Surface Tension across the skin.

Phase 5

24-to-72-Hour Cold Retard in the Fridge

🧊 Minimum 24 hours up to 72 hours maximum

  1. Line a medium round bowl (or proofing banneton) with a clean, lint-free cotton kitchen towel.

  2. Dust the towel generously with flour — ideally a 50/50 blend of bread flour and rice flour so nothing sticks (never oil!).

  3. Lay the shaped boule into the towel upside down (smooth top down, pinched seam facing UP).

  4. Fold overhanging cloth gently across the seam, slide the entire bowl inside a large clean plastic bag tied shut so fridge air never dries a skin on the dough.

  5. Refrigerate uninterrupted for 24 to 72 hours — longer cold time deepens wheat-ferment complexity, blistered crust color, and explosive oven spring.

Phase 6

How Long Does Dough Warm Up After the Fridge? (Crucial Secret)

ANSWER: ZERO MINUTES! Never let cold-retarded artisan bread sit out warming on the counter! Warm dough puddles flat and snags your blade. Going ice-cold and firm straight from fridge into a 250°C pot delivers razor-clean scoring and maximum explosive Oven Spring.
Phase 7

Baking Inside Your Home Deck Oven (Cast Iron)

🔥 Preheat pot 1 full hour at 250°C — Bake 25–30m covered @ 250°C + 15–20m uncovered @ 220°C

  1. 1 Full Hour Before Baking: Place your empty Dutch oven WITH its lid inside the oven and crank heat to max (250°C / 500°F) for a full 60 minutes until molten hot.

  2. Only once the full hour completes, pull your cold dough bowl out of the refrigerator.

  3. Invert the boule cleanly onto a sheet of parchment paper so the smooth tensioned top now faces UP, peeling the floured cloth gently off.

  4. Brush away heavy pockets of excess loose flour lightly with your palm.

  5. Scoring: Holding a razor blade (lame) or sharpest serrated knife at a shallow 45° angle, slash one confident 1 cm deep crescent cut across the dome so steam expands dramatically along your ear without blowing out randomly.

  6. Protect your hands with thick oven mitts! Pull out the screaming-hot pot, lift the loaf by the parchment sling corners, and lower straight inside.

  7. Clamp the hot lid shut immediately to trap dough steam and slide straight into the oven.

  8. Exact Two-Stage Bake Schedule

    • Stage 1 — Lid ON at 250°C (500°F): Bake 25 to 30 minutes trapped inside pure self-generated steam for maximum loft and thin crisp skin.
    • Stage 2 — Lid OFF at 220°C (430°F): Lift off the lid (watch the tall golden ear!), drop temperature to 220°C, and bake 15 to 20 minutes more until deep mahogany-caramelized and singing crisp (don't pull pale — bold dark crust equals flavor!).
Phase 100

Cooling on a Wire Rack (The Hardest Test of Willpower)

  1. Lift the finished boule onto an elevated wire cooling rack (never flat on a plate or wrapped in cloth where steam soggies the bottom crust).

NON-NEGOTIABLE 2-HOUR RULE Do NOT slice into the loaf for at least 2 full hours! Right out of the oven, the interior crumb stands near 100°C still setting starch gel; cutting hot vents steam prematurely and turns the whole crumb gummy and wet.
Congratulations! Listen to that crackling crust sing as it cools — two hours later your serrated knife opens glossy, airy artisan crumb straight out of a European bakery!
