Detroit-Style Pan Pizza with Cold Ricotta and Hot Honey

Source: Nick DiGiovanni — Nick's Kitchen (“The Ultimate Homemade Detroit Pan Pizza”)

🍕
Yield
1 large rectangular pan pizza (8 square slices / 4 to 6 servings)
⏱️
Active Prep Time
30 minutes
🧊
Inactive Proof & Fermentation
16 to 28 hours total (or 3.5–5 hrs same-day)
🔥
Bake Time
20 to 28 minutes (at 500°F / 260°C)
1. Equipment Needed Phase 1: Dough & Gluten Phase 2: Oil Pool & First Rise Phase 3: Hand-Crushed Sauce Phase 4: Cheese, Meat & Cold Proof Phase 5: Perimeter Build & Bake Phase 6: Unpanning & Cold-to-Hot Finish 4. Chef's Notes & Culinary Science

2. Ingredients (Grouped by Sub-Component)

Inactive Proof Breakdown: 2–3 hours initial warm rise + 12–24 hours overnight cold fermentation + 2–2.5 hours final warm temper; or 3.5 to 5 hours total for the same-day variation.

Component A: High-Hydration Pan Dough (75% Hydration)
Unbleached Bread Flour (high-protein, ~12.5–13.5% protein) 440 g (3⅔ cups)
Instant Yeast 3 g (1 teaspoon)
Lukewarm Water (98–105°F / 37–41°C — barely warm when tested with a clean finger) 330 g / 330 ml (1⅓ cups)
Kosher Salt (Diamond Crystal; if using fine sea salt or denser Morton kosher salt, reduce to 10 g / 2 teaspoons) 15 g (1 tablespoon)
Extra-Virgin Olive Oil (for pooling in the pan, plus an extra 1 to 2 teaspoons / 5–10 ml for coating hands and greasing the plastic wrap) 54 g / 60 ml (¼ cup) + 5–10 ml

Scaling Note: 440 g flour yields a thick, pillowy bakery-height Detroit slice in a 9×13-inch or 10×14-inch pan. If you prefer a slightly lighter, medium-thick crust in a standard 10×14-inch pan, scale proportionally at the same 73–75% hydration: 330 g [2¾ cups] bread flour + 245 g [1 cup] lukewarm water + 3 g [1 tsp] instant yeast + 10 g [2 tsp] Diamond Crystal kosher salt.

Component B: Uncooked Hand-Crushed Tomato Sauce
Whole Peeled Canned Tomatoes (preferably California plum such as Bianco DiNapoli or imported San Marzano, undrained in pure juice) 1 large can (794 g / 28 oz)
Garlic Powder 3 g (½ to ¾ teaspoon)
Dried Oregano (or 1 teaspoon / 0.5 g finely chopped fresh oregano leaves) 1.5 g (1 teaspoon)
Dried Basil (used dried inside the sauce so it withstands 500°F / 260°C heat without turning black) 1.5 g (1 teaspoon)
Crushed Red Pepper Flakes 2 to 3 g (½ teaspoon, or 1 generous pinch to taste)
Kosher Salt (adjusted to taste depending on the salt content of your canned tomatoes) 3 to 4 g (½ to ¾ teaspoon)
Freshly Cracked Black Pepper 1 g (¼ to ½ teaspoon, to taste)

Yield Note: Season the entire 28 oz can for the best flavor balance; you will spoon approximately 360 g / 1½ cups onto 1 pan pizza and store the remaining half in the refrigerator for up to 5 days or freeze for up to 3 months.

Component C: Cheese Architecture & Meat Topping
Low-Moisture Whole-Milk Mozzarella Cheese (solid block, never pre-shredded), cut into medium ½-inch (1.2 cm) cubes 170 g (6 oz)
Fontina Cheese (semi-soft wedge, firm outer rind removed), cut into slightly smaller ⅜-inch (1 cm) cubes 170 g (6 oz)
Whole Natural-Casing Pepperoni Stick, hand-sliced into medium-thick ⅛- to ¼-inch (3 to 6 mm) circular rounds 142 g (5 oz)
Parmigiano-Reggiano or Pecorino Romano Wedge (approx. ¼ cup finely grated on a Microplane) 15 to 20 g (½ to ¾ oz)
Component D: Cold & Sweet-Heat Post-Bake Finishers
Whole-Milk Fresh Ricotta Cheese (kept cold in the refrigerator until the moment of serving) 85 to 115 g (⅓ to ½ cup)
Fresh Basil Leaves, torn by hand just before serving 1 small handful (8 to 12 fresh leaves)
Extra-Virgin Olive Oil, for light post-bake finishing gloss 10 to 15 ml (2 to 3 teaspoons)
Hot Honey (chili-infused honey, such as Mike's Hot Honey), for drizzling 15 to 25 g (1 to 1½ tablespoons)

Overview: This recipe builds a classic high-hydration (75%) Detroit-style pan pizza on a high-protein bread-flour base proofed directly inside an olive-oil-coated dark hard-anodized metal pan, creating a light, open focaccia-like interior with a crisp, shallow-fried bottom crust. By reversing traditional pizza assembly—pressing cubed mozzarella and Fontina directly against the vertical pan walls beneath three stripes of uncooked hand-crushed tomato sauce—the edges render and caramelize into a dark, lacy cheese crown (frico), which is finished immediately out of the oven with chilled ricotta, fresh basil, olive oil, and hot honey.

Section 1

Equipment Needed

  • Stand Mixer with Dough Hook Attachment: Required to efficiently knead the wet, high-hydration dough until full gluten tension develops without adding excess bench flour that would toughen the crumb.
  • High-Sided Dark Hard-Anodized Rectangular Detroit Pizza Pan (10 × 14-inch / 25 × 35 cm, or a dark 9 × 13-inch / 23 × 33 cm steel baking pan): Dark anodized aluminum or seasoned blue steel absorbs radiant heat aggressively to fry the olive-oil-soaked underside and caramelize cheese directly against the tall walls without sticking. (Avoid glass pans or shiny, light-colored aluminum pans, which reflect heat and leave the bottom pale and soft).
  • Digital Kitchen Scale: Essential for measuring flour, water, and salt accurately to hit the exact 75% baker's hydration ratio.
  • Non-Reactive Glass or Ceramic Mixing Bowl & Wire Whisk: Prevents metallic off-flavors when hand-crushing and seasoning acidic raw canned tomatoes.
  • Bench Scraper or Chef's Knife: Facilitates clean, uniform cubing of the mozzarella and Fontina blocks.
  • Microplane / Fine Rasp Grater: Shaves hard aged cheese into fine, feathery strands along the sauce borders and outer perimeter.
  • Plastic Cling Wrap: Seals the pan tightly during warm proofing and overnight cold fermentation so the dough surface does not dry out and form a skin.
  • Thin Metal Offset Spatula (or Stiff Flat Metal Spatula): Slides cleanly between the baked lacy cheese wall and the pan sides to release the pizza intact without fracturing the caramelized edge.
  • Large Cutting Board & Heavy Serrated Bread Knife: A long serrated blade saws cleanly through the crunchy caramelized perimeter and blistered pepperoni without dragging toppings or crushing the airy interior crumb. (Optional: Piping bag fitted with a medium round tip if piping neat ricotta rosettes instead of spooning rustic dollops).
Phase 1

Dough Preparation & Gluten Development

🥣 Steps 1–4 · 30s dry whisk · 1 min low mix + scrape · 5 to 7 mins medium-low knead

  1. Combine Dry Ingredients

    Add 440 g (3⅔ cups) bread flour and 3 g (1 tsp) instant yeast into the bowl of a stand mixer. Briefly whisk with a wire whisk or your clean fingers for 30 seconds to distribute the yeast uniformly throughout the flour.

  2. Verify Water Temperature & Dissolve Salt

    Measure 330 g / 330 ml (1⅓ cups) lukewarm water (98–105°F / 37–41°C)—if testing by hand, dip a clean finger in to confirm it feels barely warm to body touch rather than hot, which would damage the yeast. Add 15 g (1 tbsp) Diamond Crystal kosher salt directly to the water and stir thoroughly until the salt crystals dissolve (small surface bubbles may appear as you stir).

  3. Initial Hydration & Bowl Scrape

    Attach the dough hook to the stand mixer. Pour all of the salted lukewarm water into the flour bowl and start mixing on low speed for 1 minute. Pause the mixer once early on and use a spatula to scrape down any unmixed dry flour clinging to the bowl walls and bottom so every gram hydrates evenly.

  4. Knead to the Auditory Cue

    Once a rough, shaggy dough forms, increase the mixer speed to medium-low and knead continuously for 5 to 7 minutes. Listen closely as the gluten strengthens: the wet dough will pull cleanly away from the bowl walls and emit a rhythmic, audible slapping sound against the metal bowl (often called the dough's “song”). When the mass becomes completely smooth, glossy, elastic, and cohesive around the hook, stop mixing.

Phase 2

Pan Oil Pool & First Ambient Proof

🫒 Steps 5–8 · 2 to 3 hours warm draft-free proof at 75–80°F (24–27°C)

  1. Pool Oil in the Detroit Pan

    Pour 54 g / 60 ml (¼ cup) extra-virgin olive oil directly onto the bottom of your dark Detroit-style pan. Tilt the pan and use your fingers to coat the entire floor, corners, and interior vertical walls generously—there should be a visible shallow film of olive oil across the bottom.

  2. Transfer the Dough

    Lightly rub olive oil onto your palms and fingertips so the sticky high-hydration dough cannot cling to your skin. Scrape the dough out of the mixer bowl into the center of the oiled pan and turn it over once so its entire exterior surface is coated in oil.

  3. Gentle Initial Coaxing

    Using oiled fingertips with gentle pressure, lightly press and coax the dough outward toward the edges of the pan. Do not tear or puncture through the dough, and do not force it all the way into the four corners yet—if the tight gluten resists and springs back, leave it as-is so it can relax naturally during the first rise.

  4. First Warm Rise (2 to 3 Hours)

    Cover the top of the pan tightly from rim to rim with lightly oiled plastic wrap. Place the pan in a warm, draft-free spot (75–80°F / 24–27°C—such as near a sunny windowsill or inside a turned-off oven with the interior oven light switched on) for 2 to 3 hours, until the dough has visibly expanded across the bottom of the pan, looks puffy and aerated with trapped gas bubbles, and gently jiggles like jelly when the pan is shaken.

Phase 3

Hand-Crushed Sauce Preparation

🍅 Steps 9–10 · Uncooked hand-crushed whole peeled tomatoes · Keep 360 g (1½ cups) at room temp

  1. Crush Whole Tomatoes by Hand

    Empty the entire 794 g (28 oz) can of whole peeled tomatoes along with all of its juices into a large glass or ceramic bowl. Keeping your hands low inside the bowl to prevent red juice from splattering onto your clothes or counter, squeeze and crush every whole tomato thoroughly by hand. Aim for an irregular, rustic texture containing varied small chunks of sweet pulp rather than a smooth puree (never use a blender or food processor).

  2. Season & Hold the Sauce

    To the bowl of crushed tomatoes, add 3 g (½ tsp) garlic powder, 1.5 g (1 tsp) dried oregano, 1.5 g (1 tsp) dried basil, 2–3 g (½ tsp) crushed red pepper flakes, 3–4 g kosher salt, and 1 g freshly cracked black pepper. Whisk thoroughly until completely uniform. Spoon the sauce back into its original tin or transfer to an airtight jar (keep 360 g / 1½ cups at room temperature prior to baking so cold sauce does not cool down the pizza top, and refrigerate the remainder).

Phase 4

Cheese, Meat Prep & Cold Fermentation

🧊 Steps 11–15 · 12 to 24 hrs cold ferment at 38–40°F (3–4°C) + 2 to 2.5 hrs final warm temper

  1. Cube the Mozzarella

    Place the 170 g (6 oz) block of low-moisture whole-milk mozzarella on a cutting board. Using a bench scraper or chef's knife, cut it into moderately sized ½-inch (1.2 cm) cubes (slightly irregular hand-cut blocks are ideal and create varied melt pools).

  2. Trim & Cube the Fontina Smaller

    Slice away and discard the waxy outer rind from the 170 g (6 oz) Fontina wedge. Cut the Fontina paste into slightly smaller ⅜-inch (1 cm) cubes than the mozzarella—this intentional size difference ensures the denser Fontina curds melt completely in sync with the mozzarella.

  3. Hand-Slice the Natural-Casing Pepperoni

    Slice the 142 g (5 oz) stick of natural-casing pepperoni by hand into even, sturdy ⅛- to ¼-inch (3 to 6 mm) circular discs so they curl sharply into crisp grease cups in the oven rather than burning flat.

  4. Overnight Cold Proof (Recommended Path)

    Once the dough from Step 8 has completed its 2–3 hour rise and jiggles softly, transfer the tightly sealed pan directly into the refrigerator (38–40°F / 3–4°C) to cold-ferment overnight for 12 to 24 hours.

    (Same-Day Shortcut Option: If baking on the same day, skip refrigeration; after the 2–3 hour rise, gently stretch the relaxed dough all the way into the four corners with oiled fingers, re-cover, let puff for an additional 1 to 1.5 hours until light and bubbly, and proceed directly to Step 16).

  5. Final Baking-Day Tempering

    Remove the refrigerated pan 2 to 2.5 hours before baking and leave covered in a warm, draft-free area. Allow the chilled dough to lose its chill completely, relax cleanly into all four sharp corners (coaxing gently with oiled fingertips only if needed), and rise until pillowy and filling roughly 75–80% of the height of the pan.

Phase 5

Perimeter Assembly & High-Heat Baking

🔥 Steps 16–21 · Preheat 30 mins at 500°F (260°C) · Bake 20 to 28 minutes

  1. Preheat the Oven

    Position an oven rack in the middle or lower-middle slot (second position from the bottom heating element) and preheat your oven to 500°F (260°C)—or up to 525°F (275°C) if your home oven runs cool—for at least 30 minutes before baking.

  2. Layer the Mozzarella & Build the Fontina/Mozzarella Frico Wall

    Peel off the plastic wrap carefully so you do not deflate the delicate surface air pockets. Evenly scatter the larger mozzarella cubes across the center dough surface, then distribute the smaller Fontina cubes throughout the gaps between them. Finally, press an unbroken ring of Fontina and mozzarella cubes firmly and directly flush against all four vertical metal walls of the pan from corner to corner, leaving zero bare dough exposed along the perimeter.

  3. Arrange the Pepperoni

    Nestle the hand-sliced pepperoni rounds evenly across the surface between and atop the cheese blocks right out into the four corners so every square slice gets generous coverage (optionally tucking half the slices just beneath the upper cheese cubes and leaving half exposed on top for maximum cupping and char).

  4. Apply Three Parallel Sauce Racing Stripes

    Do not spread sauce edge-to-edge across the pizza. Using a large spoon, ladle 360 g (1½ cups) of the room-temperature hand-crushed tomato sauce in three continuous parallel stripes running lengthwise down the pan (one down the left third, one down the center, and one down the right third). Then spoon a thin trace of extra sauce directly along the bare metal perimeter edge where the cheese wall meets the pan.

  5. Shave the Grated Cheese Crown

    Using a Microplane or fine rasp grater, shave a light, even dusting of 15–20 g Parmigiano-Reggiano (or Pecorino Romano) specifically along the borders of the tomato sauce stripes and all around the outer perimeter edge of the pan.

  6. Bake to Caramelized Frico Perfection

    Place the pan in the 500°F (260°C) oven and bake for 20 to 28 minutes (begin checking at 18 minutes if using a thinner 330 g dough batch or high-output convection oven, rotating the pan 180 degrees halfway through for even browning). Bake until the center cheese is bubbling and blistered golden-brown, the pepperoni slices curl into dark crisp cups, and the entire perimeter cheese wall has melted down and caramelized into a deep mahogany, nearly blackened lacy crust (frico).

Phase 6

Unpanning, Cold-to-Hot Finishing & Service

🍯 Steps 22–27 · 2–4 min pan set · Ice-cold Ricotta + fresh basil + olive oil + Hot Honey

  1. Rest Briefly in Pan

    Remove the pan from the oven and let rest undisturbed on a heatproof surface for 2 to 4 minutes. This brief pause lets the molten perimeter cheese set from liquid oil into a rigid, crunchy lace shell so it will not tear when lifted.

  2. Release & Unpan

    Run a thin metal offset spatula firmly all the way around all four interior pan walls to separate the caramelized frico crust from the metal. Lift one corner to verify that the olive-oil-fried bottom crust is golden-brown and audibly crisp, then slide a wide spatula cleanly underneath and transfer the entire pizza onto a wooden cutting board (optionally resting on a wire rack for 60 seconds first if you want zero steam trapped beneath the crust).

  3. Dollop Ice-Cold Ricotta

    Immediately while the pizza is still scorching hot, use a spoon (or piping bag) to drop generous, rustic clouds of well-chilled fresh whole-milk ricotta (85–115 g / ⅓–½ cup) across the hot zones of the pizza—aiming for at least one cold creamy dollop on every square slice.

  4. Scatter Fresh Torn Basil

    Tear the 8 to 12 fresh basil leaves cleanly by hand right over the pizza and scatter them across the molten cheese and red sauce stripes so the residual heat releases their essential oils immediately without cooking them dark.

  5. Drizzle Olive Oil & Hot Honey

    Lightly thread 2 to 3 teaspoons (10–15 ml) finishing extra-virgin olive oil over the top for sheen and fruitiness, followed immediately by an intersecting zigzag drizzle of hot honey (15–25 g / 1–1½ tbsp) across the crispy pepperoni cups, cold ricotta, and charred frico edges.

  6. Slice & Serve Immediately

    Using a heavy, long serrated bread knife with a gentle vertical sawing stroke, slice cleanly down through the crunchy lacy crust in a grid (1 cut lengthwise × 3 cuts crosswise for 8 thick square slices) and serve right away while the contrast between the steaming crisp crust and chilled ricotta is at its peak.

Section 4

Chef's Notes & Culinary Science (Why It Works)

  1. Why High-Protein Bread Flour + 75% Hydration Creates Signature Chew

    Standard all-purpose flour (~10–11% protein) forms a weaker gluten matrix that tends to tear or bake up cakey at high water levels. Bread flour (12.5–13.5% protein) builds elastic gluten sheets strong enough to trap large steam pockets generated by a 75% hydration dough at 500°F (260°C), creating a light, open honeycomb interior that stays airy underneath heavy cheese and meat toppings.

  2. Why Salt Dissolves in Water First

    Dissolving kosher salt completely into the lukewarm water before it touches the flour guarantees uniform ionic distribution across the entire gluten network from second one, preventing localized pockets of undissolved salt crystals from dehydrating yeast cells or cutting through developing gluten strands during kneading.

  3. The Physics of Overnight Cold Fermentation (12–24 Hours)

    Chilling the proofed dough at 38–40°F (3–4°C) slows Saccharomyces cerevisiae gas production to a crawl while allowing flour enzymes (amylases and proteases) and lactic acid bacteria to keep breaking complex starches and proteins down into simple sugars, amino acids, and organic acids. The result is deeper, more complex wheat flavor, superior Maillard crust browning, and thoroughly relaxed gluten that stretches effortlessly into sharp rectangular pan corners without snapping back.

  4. Differential Cheese Cubing & The Frico Crown

    Never use pre-shredded bagged mozzarella, which is dusted with cellulose or potato starch anti-caking agents that block smooth fat coalescence and burn dry. Cubing whole-milk mozzarella alongside slightly smaller cubes of richer, firmer Fontina equalizes melt timing. Packing cubes tightly against dark anodized pan walls lets rendered butterfat drip down the 500°F metal seam—shallow-frying the dough edges while milk solids and lactose caramelize directly against the pan wall into a bitter-sweet, audibly crunchy lace (frico).

  5. Reverse Assembly & Uncooked Hand-Crushed Tomatoes

    Laying cheese directly on raw dough underneath striped sauce creates a waterproof lipid barrier that prevents wet tomato juices from soaking into the dough and leaving a dense, soggy “gum line.” Crushing whole peeled tomatoes by hand avoids both the calcium chloride firming salts added to canned diced tomatoes and the seed bitterness caused by high-speed blender blades.

  6. Botanical Heat Protection at 500°F (260°C)

    Fresh sweet basil leaves contain delicate volatile aromatics and high moisture that oxidize and char black within minutes at 500°F. Seasoning the raw sauce with dried oregano and dried basil allows safe herbal infusion during baking, while adding raw torn basil post-oven preserves its bright green color and fresh anise-mint aroma.

  7. Hot-to-Cold & Salty-to-Sweet Sensory Architecture

    Plating cold refrigerator ricotta directly onto molten 500°F bubbling cheese creates an immediate temperature contrast on the tongue in every bite, while spicy-sweet hot honey cuts across the intense savoriness of rendered natural-casing pepperoni fat and charred cheese edges.
