New York Egg Cream

New York Egg Cream — New York City's iconic soda-fountain drink famously contains neither egg nor cream. Just three cold ingredients: milk, seltzer water, and chocolate syrup. This archive preserves the winning The Spruce Eats specification (by Kristina Vanni, updated May 27, 2022, recipe-tested by Diana Andrews; rated 3.6/5 across 53 votes), chosen head-to-head over Allrecipes because it specifies a freezer-frosted glass, exact ratios, technical pour order for a pure white foam head, and authentic Brooklyn Fox's U-Bet chocolate syrup. Two rival origin legends endure: Brooklyn candy-shop owner Louis Auster in the 1890s, or Lower East Side shopkeeper Hymie Bell in the 1920s — whose original drink reportedly contained egg and cream until Great Depression soda jerks cut the egg and swapped milk for cream.

Ingredients

Makes one 12-oz glass. All ingredients must be thoroughly chilled and the serving glass frosted in the freezer beforehand.

New York Egg Cream
Ice-cold milk1 cup
Ice-cold seltzer water¼ cup
Chocolate syrup (traditionally Fox's U-Bet)2 tbsp
Equipment
12-oz fountain glass (pre-frosted in freezer)1 glass
Long-handled spoon & strawFor stirring & serving

Method

Making the Egg Cream (Winning Method)

Glass must be freezer-cold; mix and serve immediately while effervescent

  1. 1. Frost the Serving Glass

    Gather your ingredients and place a 12-ounce serving glass in the freezer until frosted. The glass must be ice-cold before building and serving the Egg Cream.

  2. 2. Pour the Cold Milk

    Pour the ice-cold milk into the frosted glass.

  3. 3. Add Seltzer to Build the White Head

    Pour the cold seltzer briskly into the milk until a tall, pure white foam head rises right to the rim of the glass.

  4. 4. Fold in Chocolate Syrup & Serve

    Spoon the chocolate syrup down into the glass and stir briskly at the bottom to blend the body while keeping the white foam crown intact. Serve immediately with a straw.

Why Pour Order Matters Adding seltzer to plain milk before chocolate syrup guarantees a snowy white foam head — documented in the source as authentic Brooklyn Style. Mixing chocolate syrup into the milk first and adding seltzer afterward turns the foam brown, known as Bronx Style.
Style Guide

Brooklyn Style vs. Bronx Style

  1. Brooklyn Style (This Recipe): Seltzer hits the cold milk before the chocolate syrup goes in — producing a crisp white foam crown over a dark chocolate body.

  2. Bronx Style: Chocolate syrup and milk are stirred together first and topped with seltzer — producing a light brown chocolate foam head.

Traditional Syrup Authentic New York Egg Creams use Fox's U-Bet chocolate syrup — the historic Brooklyn staple many New Yorkers insist makes or breaks a real Egg Cream. Standard chocolate syrup works in a pinch, keeping the exact 2-tablespoon measure.
History & Notes

Origin Lore, Tester Review & Nutrition (From Source)

  1. Two Rival Origin Stories

    In early 20th-century New York, nearly every city block boasted a corner candy store with a marble soda fountain pouring chocolate egg creams all day. One tradition credits Brooklyn candy-store proprietor Louis Auster in the 1890s; another credits Hymie Bell on Manhattan's Lower East Side in the 1920s. In Bell's telling, the drink originally included whole egg and heavy cream until Great Depression cost-cutting eliminated the egg and substituted milk for cream. If Bell's account holds, “Egg Cream” reflects its lost original ingredients; if Auster's holds, the name comes from the snowy white foam head resembling beaten egg whites. As the old New York saying went: “Imagining a candy store without an egg cream is as hard as imagining the Earth without gravity.”

    “This egg cream came together effortlessly and was even better to drink. Sipping ice-cold milk and seltzer swirled with rich chocolate syrup felt like a nostalgic trip back to a simpler New York.”
