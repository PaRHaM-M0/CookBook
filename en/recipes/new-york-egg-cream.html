New York Egg Cream

Iconic early-20th-century NYC candy-store classic containing neither egg nor cream: just ice-cold milk, cold seltzer, and chocolate syrup layered in a frosted 12-oz glass for a snowy Brooklyn-style foam head.

Yield
1 Drink (12-oz Frosted Glass)
Prep & Total
2 Minutes
Nutrition
231 kcal · 5 g Fat · 37 g Carbs · 9 g Protein

Ingredients

By Kristina Vanni, tested by Diana Andrews (The Spruce Eats).

Classic New York Egg Cream
Cold milk1 cup
Cold seltzer water¼ cup
Chocolate syrup (traditionally Fox's U-Bet)2 tbsp
12-oz serving glass (chilled ice-cold in freezer) + Straw1 set
Method

4-Step Brooklyn Build

  1. Frost the Glass

    Chill a 12-oz serving glass in the freezer until ice cold.

  2. Cold Milk

    Pour 1 cup cold milk into the frosted glass.

  3. Build the Snowy Head

    Pour ¼ cup cold seltzer in until the white foam rises right to the rim.

  4. Syrup & Gentle Stir

    Spoon 2 tbsp chocolate syrup in and stir gently to combine while preserving the foam head. Serve immediately with a straw!

Technique

Brooklyn White Head vs. Bronx Brown Head

  1. Brooklyn-Style (Above): Adding seltzer to cold milk before chocolate syrup produces a pure snowy-white foam crown.

  2. Bronx-Style: Mixing chocolate syrup and milk together first and adding seltzer afterward builds a rich chocolate-tinted brown foam head.

Lore & Verdict

NYC Soda Fountain Lore & Why Spruce Eats Won

  1. Origin Debate

    Attributed either to Brooklyn candy-shop owner Louis Auster in the 1890s (named for the beaten-egg-white-like foam head) or Lower East Side shopkeeper Hymie Bell in the 1920s (whose original egg-and-cream drink dropped eggs and swapped milk during the Great Depression).

  2. Why The Spruce Eats Beat Allrecipes

    The Spruce Eats specifies exact chocolate syrup measurement, mandatory glass frosting, Fox's U-Bet heritage, and Brooklyn vs. Bronx foam mechanics, outperforming Allrecipes' unmeasured single-step shortcut.
