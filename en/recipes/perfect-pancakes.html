Overview: Homemade pancakes are truly a beautiful thing in the morning — the only real issue is deciding which recipe to stick to: the tall, airy Japanese Soufflé Pancake, or the traditional golden American "flapjack". This page keeps both of Joshua Weissman's formulas intact side by side: one whole-egg batter that rests five minutes and griddles into fluffy golden rounds, and one meringue-based batter that is piped high and steam-cooked under a tight lid into jiggly soufflé towers.

Foundation

Two Styles, One Morning

Each style builds structure in a completely different way — knowing which is which makes the steps click:

  1. Style A — The American "Flapjack" (Chemical Lift)

    A single whole egg, plenty of milk, and the one-two punch of baking powder + baking soda do all the lifting. The batter is whisked just until mostly smooth (a few lumps are fine), rested for 5 minutes, then spooned onto a medium-heat skillet for golden, bouncy, fluffy rounds.

  2. Style B — The Japanese Soufflé Pancake (Mechanical Lift)

    Eggs are separated: the yolks build a light base batter with sifted cake flour, while the whites are whipped with sugar into stiff, glossy meringue and folded in four additions. The airy batter is piped tall and cooked slowly with steam under a tight lid — structure comes from trapped air, not from a big dose of leavener.

  3. What They Share

    A nonstick skillet, cooking spray (or butter) for greasing, gentle handling, and the final rule of both recipes: serve immediately — butter first, then the toppings of that style.

Hardware

Equipment Needed

  • Two mixing bowls + balloon whisk
  • Digital kitchen scale and measuring cups / spoons
  • For Style A: ¼-cup measure or scoop for portioning batter, nonstick skillet, spatula
  • For Style B: Electric mixer (for the meringue), fine sieve for the cake flour, piping bag with a round tip (or a plain bag with the tip cut off), nonstick skillet with a tight-fitting lid
  • Paper towel (to wipe off excess cooking spray in Style B)
  • Optional upgrade: Smoking gun (such as Breville® The Smoking Gun) + hickory chips for smoking the maple syrup
Style A

All-American Pancake — 12 Fluffy Flapjacks

🥞 Steps 1–5 · Prep 15 min · Cook 24 min · 5-minute batter rest · Medium heat

  1. Combine Egg and Milk

    In a small bowl, whisk together the 1 egg and 1.5 cups (386g) milk until evenly combined.

  2. Whisk the Dry Ingredients

    In a separate bowl, whisk together the 2 cups (300g) flour, ¼ cup (62g) sugar, 1 tsp (4g) salt, 1 Tbsp (12g) baking powder and ¼ tsp (1g) baking soda.

  3. Form the Batter and Rest

    Whisk the wet ingredients into the dry until a batter begins to form. While whisking, pour in the 2 Tbsp (21g) melted butter and continue to whisk until a mostly smooth batter forms; it's ok if there are a few lumps. Let the batter rest for about 5 minutes.

  4. Griddle the Pancakes

    Preheat a nonstick skillet over medium heat and grease with cooking spray or butter. Once hot, spoon in ¼-cup dollops of the batter, making sure to leave space between each to have room to spread. Cook for 2–3 minutes or until golden brown on the bottom. Flip and cook for an additional 1–3 minutes, or until golden brown and cooked through but still bouncy to the touch. Repeat with the rest of the batter.

  5. Butter and Maple Syrup

    Top with butter and a drizzle of maple syrup to serve (optional: smoke the maple syrup with hickory chips in a smoking gun).

Style B

Japanese Soufflé Pancake — 3 Tall Jiggly Pancakes

🍮 Steps 1–9 · Prep 15 min · Cook 15 min · Meringue + steam-lid method · Low heat

  1. Build the Yolk Batter

    Add the 2 egg yolks to a medium bowl and whisk together with the 1 Tbsp (14g) milk and 1 tsp (3g) vanilla extract. Sift in the ¼ cup (30g) cake flour, ¾ tsp (3g) baking powder and ½ tsp (1g) salt and whisk to combine.

  2. Whip the Meringue

    In a separate medium bowl, whisk the egg whites with an electric mixer. Once soft peaks form, slowly stream in the 2.5 Tbsp (35g) sugar and continue to whip until stiff, glossy peaks form.

  3. Fold in Four Additions

    Fold in a ¼ of the meringue into the batter until fully incorporated. Repeat this 3 more times until all of the meringue is incorporated. The batter will be smooth and very fluffy; make sure not to overmix and deflate the egg whites.

  4. Load the Piping Bag

    Transfer the batter to a piping bag fitted with a round tip — or without a tip, and then cut the tip off the piping bag.

  5. Preheat the Pan

    Heat a nonstick skillet that you have a tight-fitting lid for over low heat until hot. Spray with cooking spray and wipe off any excess with a paper towel.

  6. Pipe, Steam and Cook the First Layer

    Pipe two evenly sized mounds of batter into the pan, using about half of the batter. Make sure to leave enough space between each pancake. Add a spoonful of water to the blank part of the pan and immediately cover. Let steam and cook for 5 minutes.

  7. Stack the Second Layer

    Lift the lid and pipe another large mound of batter on top of the two pancakes. Add another spoonful of water, cover, and cook for an additional 3–4 minutes.

  8. Flip and Finish Cooking

    Remove the lid and carefully lift the edge of one of the pancakes with a spatula and gently let the pancake roll over onto its other side. Repeat with the second pancake. Add another spoonful of water, and cover. Cook for 3–4 minutes or until golden brown on the bottom and cooked through; it's important to make sure the pancakes are cooked through or they will deflate when removed from the pan. Remove the pancakes to a plate. Repeat with the remaining batter.

  9. Butter, Cream and Powdered Sugar

    Top the pancakes with butter, whipped cream, a dusting of powdered sugar and maple syrup. Serve!

Technique

Key Technique Notes

  1. Why a Few Lumps Are Perfectly Fine (Style A)

    The recipe stops whisking at "mostly smooth" on purpose: overworking the batter develops gluten and turns flapjacks tough. The 5-minute rest lets the flour hydrate and the leaveners begin working, so the pancakes puff evenly on the skillet.

  2. Why the Meringue Is Folded in Four Additions (Style B)

    The first quarter of the meringue lightens the yolk batter so the rest folds in without collapsing. Folding in stages — and stopping the moment everything is incorporated — keeps the air that makes the pancakes tall; overmixing deflates the egg whites.

  3. Why Water and a Tight Lid (Style B)

    The spoonful of water instantly turns the covered pan into a gentle steamer, setting the tall structure before the bottoms can over-brown. The flip happens only in the final stage — and the pancakes must be cooked through before leaving the pan, or they will deflate on the plate.

  4. Yes, Really — 1 Tbsp of Milk (Style B)

    The Japanese batter uses only 1 Tbsp (14g) of milk. This is not a typo: the liquid in the batter is carried almost entirely by the eggs, and too much milk would weigh the meringue down.

  5. Optional: Smoked Maple Syrup

    For a steakhouse-level touch on Style A, smoke the maple syrup with hickory chips in a smoking gun (such as Breville® The Smoking Gun) before drizzling — everything else in the recipe stays exactly the same.
