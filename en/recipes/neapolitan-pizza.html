Phase 1

Dough Mixing, Strengthening & 72h Cold Ferment

72 hours before baking day

  1. Initial Mixing Sequence (Official AVPN Water-First Order)

    Calculated cleanly for 2 × ~270 g balls at 70% hydration, starting strictly from water:

    • Water & Salt: Zero your mixing bowl on a digital scale, pour in 221 g water (16–22°C), add 9 g fine sea salt, and stir until completely dissolved.
    • Protective Buffer Slurry: Whisk in 32 g flour (~10% of total flour) to form a thin batter that shields the yeast from prolonged direct salt contact.
    • Yeast: Dissolve 0.9 g fresh yeast (or 0.3 g instant dry yeast — exact 1:3 AVPN ratio) into the slurry; never let yeast sit directly in concentrated brine for more than 5 minutes.
    • Remaining Flour: Gradually rain in the remaining 283 g flour over low speed (or mix with a wooden spoon and wet hand) until no dry patches remain and a single shaggy, tacky mass forms. Do not add oil or sugar.
    Official ReferenceFollows the AVPN True Neapolitan Pizza Regulations water-first method, with yeast reduced for a 72-hour cold ferment.
    Preferment AlternativePrefer an airy contemporary crust built on an overnight preferment? Use our standalone 72-Hour Poolish Pizza Dough, then pick right back up at Phase 2 below.

  2. Initial Rest (Autolyse — 30 Minutes)

    Cover the bowl tightly and rest at room temperature for 30 minutes so the flour hydrates fully and gluten begins relaxing effortlessly.

  3. Stretch & Fold Sets (3 to 4 Sets, Every 30–45 Minutes)

    Wet your hands lightly, lift one edge of the dough upward until it resists without tearing, fold it over the center, rotate the bowl 90°, and repeat on all 4 sides. Cover and rest 30–45 minutes between sets. After the 3rd set, the dough will feel taut, smooth, and blistered (perform a 4th set only if still slack).

  4. 72-Hour Bulk Cold Fermentation

    Round the dough smoothly into a sealed container or plastic-wrapped bowl and place immediately in the refrigerator for 72 hours for deep enzymatic flavor without sourness.

Phase 2

Preparing the Sauces

1 day before or morning of baking
Golden Rule of Pizza SauceLess is always more! Excess sauce is the #1 cause of a soggy, gum-lined center.

  1. Method 1: Fresh Tomatoes (Pro Standard)

    Score an "X" on the base of 400 g ripe Roma/San Marzano tomatoes, blanch in boiling water for 30–60 seconds, shock immediately in ice water, peel, halve, and scoop out all watery seeds until you have 200–240 g pure meaty pulp (weigh it!). Crush strictly by clean hand — never use a blender, which aerates and turns pulp watery. Season lightly with salt, hand-torn basil, and at most ½ tsp olive oil (or omit completely). Chill.

  2. Method 2: High-Quality Paste Backup

    Whisk 2 heaped tbsp quality tomato paste with 4–6 tbsp cold water spoon by spoon to the consistency of stirred yogurt, then fold in salt, torn fresh basil, 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, and just a tiny pinch of sugar if sharp.

  3. Optional Italian Dipping Sauce (For the Crust)

    Whisk 3 tbsp mayonnaise, 1 tbsp French dressing, and 1 tsp ketchup. Rub 1 tsp dried thyme and ½ tsp dried oregano/basil between your palms to release essential oils and add with optional 1 mashed garlic clove, black pepper, and ½ tsp white vinegar or lemon juice. Whisking rapidly, stream in 1 tbsp neutral oil as a thin thread until glossy and emulsified. Chill 4–5 hours (or overnight).

Phase 3

Topping Mise en Place

Luxury Pizzeria Toppings ReferenceWant artisan upgrades beyond the classic build below — from smoked marinated turkey bacon to Guanciale, Burrata, and Taleggio? Explore our full Pizza Toppings Guide.

  1. Cheeses (Mozzarella + Danish Esrom)

    Portion 160–200 g Mozzarella (80–100 g per pie) and dice 60–80 g Danish Esrom into uniform 1 cm cubes (30–40 g per pie) for rich pockets of flavor. Store sealed with all air pressed out.

  2. Mushrooms

    Keep dry in a paper bag in the fridge (never wash ahead). Slice crosswise into thin "skull" cross-sections; limit strictly to 8–12 slices per pie so moisture can evaporate instantaneously.

  3. Salami / Pepperoni

    Count out 10–15 slices per pie on a plate ready to grab.

    Artisan Cure SwapSwap salami for cubed homemade Porchetta-Infused Bacon.

  4. Transfer Hardware

    Cut 4 parchment squares wider than your pies and 2 heavy-duty foil sheets (~45×45 cm).

Phase 4

Dividing, Balling & Room-Temperature Tempering

2.5 to 3 hours before baking

  1. Turn the cold bulk dough onto a lightly dusted surface and scale 270 g dough balls for 28–30 cm Neapolitan pies. (Any extra scaled balls freeze cleanly for up to 3 months in lightly oiled, air-free zip bags.)

  2. Shape into smooth, taut balls, space apart in a lightly oiled tray, cover airtight with plastic plus a damp towel, and let temper untouched at room temperature for 2 to 3 hours until soft, pillowy, and doubled.

Phase 5

Supercharging the Home Oven

60 to 90 minutes before baking

  1. Position your pizza stone, steel, or inverted heavy cast-iron pan on the highest rack and preheat the oven at absolute maximum temperature (typically 250°C / 485°F) for 1 to 1.5 full hours without opening the door.

Phase 6

Hand-Stretching & Precision Assembly

Strict RuleNever touch the dough with a rolling pin — it crushes 72 hours of gas pockets into a dense cracker.

  1. Coat, Piano-Press & Gravity Stretch

    Coat both sides of one dough ball in a flour/semolina mix. Press gently from the center outward with fingertips ("playing piano"), leaving a 1–2 cm untouched outer rim to puff into the Cornicione. Drape over your knuckles and rotate gently using gravity until 28–30 cm wide with a translucent-thin center. Pinch shut any tiny tear immediately.

  2. Early Transfer to Parchment

    Lay the stretched base onto parchment before adding a single drop of sauce.

  3. Millimeter-Accurate Topping Build

    Spiral 60–80 g sauce (2–3 tbsp max) leaving the 1–2 cm rim bare → scatter 80–100 g Mozzarella loosely with breathing gaps → dot 30–40 g cubed Esrom → place 10–15 salami slices → finish with 8–12 mushroom slices.

Phase 7

Back-to-Back Baking & Rapid Swap

  1. Never bake two pies at once in a home oven. Slide Pie #1 (on its parchment) onto the blazing hot stone and bake ~12 minutes until bubbling and blistered.

  2. While Pie #1 bakes, stretch and top Pie #2 completely on its parchment.

  3. Execute a sub-5-second swap: pull Pie #1 straight onto a wire cooling rack (so steam escapes underneath), slide Pie #2 onto the stone immediately, and slam the oven door shut.

Phase 8

Thermal Foil Tent Engineering (For Transport)

  1. Never wrap foil flat against hot cheese. Lay a 45×45 cm heavy foil sheet down with dry parchment centered inside, land the hot pizza inside, bring opposite foil edges high above the center, fold down like a chip bag leaving an air dome above the toppings, crimp the sides shut, and poke 3–4 tiny vent holes strictly at the top peak so steam escapes without killing crispness.

Phase 9

VIP Car Loading Protocol

  1. Preheat the car cabin for 5 minutes. Slide the passenger seat back, lay a rigid flat board across the seat base so the pies stay level, line with a thick blanket/towel, set the vented foil tents flat, and drape another thick towel loosely over the sides while leaving the top steam vents clear. Arrives hot and crisp 30–40 minutes later.
