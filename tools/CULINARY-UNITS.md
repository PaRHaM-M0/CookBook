# Culinary unit conversion

`assets/culinary-units.js` supplies the recipe ingredient-card control. Mass is converted only to mass, volume only to volume; no ingredient density is assumed. Counts, pinches and “to taste” are not converted. A fluid ounce (`fl oz`) is a volume, while an ounce (`oz`) is a mass.

The library uses grams, millilitres and millimetres as base units. US liquid measures are distinct from UK imperial and metric measures: a US cup is 236.5882365 mL, an imperial gallon is 4.54609 L, and a metric cup is 250 mL. Unless otherwise specified, recipe cups, tablespoons, teaspoons and gallons are interpreted as US customary. The calculator also exposes explicit UK and metric unit IDs. Temperature conversion is affine; cooking instructions and temperatures are not converted or scaled by the ingredient control.

Ingredient text is parsed for recognizable quantities and units; unknown text remains as written. Conversions are displayed with `≈` and four significant digits. When an ingredient has multiple original measurements, each remains present and is converted independently; small differences may reflect the source's rounding or ingredient-specific weight. This avoids replacing an author's weight with a guessed volume-to-weight equivalence. Fractional numbers, ranges and Persian digits are supported. The original markup is retained as the source when toggling or changing scale, preventing cumulative rounding.

The custom scale accepts values from 0.01 to 100 and persists in the page URL. Temperatures, percentages and cooking dimensions are not deliberately scaled; durations and equipment sizes do not automatically change. Method and yield quantities marked with `data-scale-quantity` also scale; specially authored 0.5× and 2× alternatives use `data-scale-half` / `data-scale-double`. Free-form content can be ambiguous: prefer structured and explicitly marked amounts when adding or revising recipes, and test linked method quantities as well as ingredients. Some source descriptions contain qualitative amounts (e.g., a pinch), which cannot be reliably converted.

References: [NIST SP 811, Appendix B](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors), [NIST Handbook 44, Appendix C](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/nist-handbook-44-current-edition).
